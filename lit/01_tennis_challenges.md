# Domain 01 — Tennis Hawk-Eye line-call challenges as a decision problem and learning story

Reader notes for the SSAC27 ABS-challenge paper. Access notes: full texts read for AEKM 2012 (IER PDF), Anbarci-Lee-Ulker 2016 (Durham repository PDF), Almog-Gauriot-Page-Martin 2024/25 (arXiv v3), Bassetti et al. 2019 (Padova WP PDF), Nadimpalli 2010 UT thesis (full), Sackmann blog posts, ITF/Grand-Slam procedure text. Abstract-only (paywalled or proxy-blocked): Nadimpalli & Hasenbein 2013 JQAS, Clarke & Norman 2012 JORS, Mather 2008 Proc R Soc B (Crossref abstract + secondary), Whitney et al. 2008 (Academia.edu extract), Kovalchik-Sackmann-Reid 2017 (OpenAlex abstract), Carboch et al. 2016 (OpenAlex abstract), Collins & Evans 2008/2011.

## 1. Domain summary

Tennis introduced player-initiated Hawk-Eye review in March 2006 (Miami; US Open Aug 2006; Australian Open and Wimbledon 2007), with a rule that is structurally almost identical to MLB's 2026 ABS challenge: a small, *renewable* budget of review rights (originally 2 unsuccessful challenges per set, harmonized to 3 in March 2008; +1 in a tiebreak; unlimited *successful* challenges; reset every set / every 12 games in advantage sets; only the player, immediately, on a point-ending shot or after stopping play). This created a clean field laboratory that economists and OR researchers used to (i) solve the optimal-stopping/dynamic-programming problem (challenge iff subjective probability of overturn q exceeds a state-dependent cutoff q\*(s,c) = option value / (option value + stakes)); (ii) test whether professionals behave near-optimally (AEKM 2012: yes, ~97% of attainable value, but systematically *too conservative*, with success rates falling when stakes rise and when option value falls); (iii) measure perceptual limits of players vs officials (Mather 2008: both <40 mm SD, judges more reliable; Whitney et al. 2008: 84% of judge errors are "out" calls on balls that were in); (iv) study gender/psychology in challenge use (Anbarci et al. 2016; Kovalchik et al. 2017); and (v) study how *review itself changes the officials* (Almog et al.: overall error rate down 8% but a shift toward calling close balls "in", i.e., away from the error type that gets publicly overturned; Bassetti et al.: line judges make more "definitive" out-calls once reversible). The challenge era is now closing: ATP (all events, incl. clay, from 2025), WTA, Australian Open (2021), US Open (2022) and Wimbledon (2025) use ELC Live with no line judges and no line-call challenges; Roland Garros alone keeps human line judges (2026). This makes tennis a completed natural experiment—challenge system → full automation—that MLB is now traversing in the opposite order at Triple-A (full ABS 2023-24 → challenge system) and MLB (challenge system 2026). Nothing in the tennis literature does what we plan: no paper estimates *revealed thresholds by role* (tennis has one decision-maker), none exploits within-season learning with per-pitch ground truth, and none runs rule-design counterfactuals on real data (only Clarke & Norman's DP-based "59%" figure and Sackmann's simulations).

## 2. Per-work entries

### 2.1 Abramitzky, Einav, Kolkowitz & Mill (2012) — READ IN FULL (anchor)
- Citation: Ran Abramitzky, Liran Einav, Shimon Kolkowitz, Roy Mill, "On the Optimality of Line Call Challenges in Professional Tennis," *International Economic Review* 53(3): 939–964, Aug 2012. DOI 10.1111/j.1468-2354.2012.00706.x. PDF: https://web.stanford.edu/~leinav/pubs/IER2012.pdf
- Rule in their sample (p. 941 + fn 9): "In most tournaments covered by our data, players were endowed with two opportunities to challenge per set"; unsuccessful challenge costs one opportunity, successful challenge "won the point and did not lose the challenge opportunity"; +1 at 6-6 tiebreak; reset each set; only the player who lost the point may challenge; Hawk-Eye verdict shown on big screen in 20–30 s. Not used on clay.
- Data (Sec. 3): ATP umpire sheets, 35 tournaments (Masters-1000 level, non-clay), 2006–2008 = first three years of Hawk-Eye. 2,784 men's-singles challenges by 179 players in 741 matches; 3,343 incl. women (men = 83%). Baseline sample after cleaning: 2,008 challenges, 158 players, 479 matches, 71,555 points. 106,166 points played → 2.6% challenged. Each record: challenger, original call, Hawk-Eye verdict, Hawk-Eye distance (mm) to line. Overall success rate 38%. 92% of challenges within 100 mm of the line; distance mean −16.4 mm, SD 59.7 mm (Fig. 1). 18% of challenges made with a single challenge left; 61% of those failed (11% of sample ended with 0 challenges). Only 37 challenged points had zero option value (last point of set); 11 (29.7%) succeeded → calibrates G(0)=0.295. Crucially, they observe *only challenged points*, not the unchallenged near-misses (p. 942).
- Model (Sec. 4): V(s,c) = win probability at score s with c challenges; points i.i.d. Bernoulli with ps (serve) and pr (return), estimated from realized match fractions (Klaassen & Magnus 2001). Optimal rule (Eq. 2, p. 945): challenge iff q > q\*(s,c) ≡ [V(s,c) − V(s,c−1)] / {[V(s⊕,c) − V(s,c)] + [V(s,c) − V(s,c−1)]}. Bellman with challenge (Eqs. 4–5): after losing a point with belief q, V\* = max{V(s,c), qV(s⊕,c) + (1−q)V(s,c−1)}. Assumption 1: F(q) is a mixture of a mass 1−p_close at q=0 and a continuous part, invariant to (s,c) (p. 947). Selection trick (p. 940): "a higher propensity to challenge implies challenging less conservatively, thereby leading to lower success rates" → success rate is a monotone signature of the threshold, so unobserved unchallenged calls are not needed for qualitative tests.
- Estimation/findings (Secs. 5–6): Table 1: success 40.5% early vs 34.9% late in set (p=.010); 56.4% at 40-0/0-40 vs 33.3% at 40-40 (p=.003); 38.7% regular games vs 26.5% tiebreaks (p=.002). Reversing a call moves match-win probability by 5.17 pp on average (13.86 pp in tiebreaks vs 4.38 pp in regular games; Table 2). Table 3 LPM (dep. var. = 100·success): importance −0.63\*\*\* (0.16); challenges remaining −4.7\* to −14.8\*\*\*; expected points left in set +0.18\*\*\* (0.06); 1 SD of importance lowers success 3–4 pp; 10th→90th percentile lowers it 6–8 pp; "Success rates increase by about 1% for every 7–12 additional points that are expected to be played in the set" (p. 952). Winning players have higher success rates (challenge less). Structural exercise (Sec. 6, linear G, p_close=2%): challenge technology worth +1.6 pp match-win probability on average; observed behavior captures 96.8% of the optimal gain (V_no=0.4781, V_opt=0.4941, V_obs=0.4936; random challenging captures 74.5%); robustness range 89–100% (Table 5). Direction of deviation: "players are too conservative and tend to challenge too little" (p. 956); "excess propensity to challenge" −0.064 baseline. No systematic heterogeneity by player/point found ("possibly due to lack of statistical power"); no learning-over-2006-08 test.
- Quotes: "The decision to challenge, which is simple to characterize, trades off reversing the umpire's call against losing subsequent challenge opportunities" (abstract). "Players have some additional costs of challenging (beyond the option value…) that are absolute in nature and invariant to the actual stakes… criticized for making too many unsuccessful challenges" (pp. 953–4). "We do not claim that tennis players actually solve our model… but at least they develop heuristics that lead to 'near-rational' behavior" (p. 961). Contrast with Levitt (2006) and Romer (2006): fast, frequent, public feedback and no agency costs explain near-optimality (pp. 940, 961).
- Relation to our pillars: this *is* our DP (renewable token, finite horizon, stakes vs option value) and our "revealed threshold via success rate" identification; their "psychic cost of a failed challenge" is our candidate explanation for baseball under-challenging. Left undone: unchallenged near-misses (we have every pitch's true location), multiple roles (catcher/batter/pitcher; team-level budget → agency/coordination), learning across a season, opponent's token stock, rule counterfactuals.

### 2.2 Nadimpalli & Hasenbein (2013) + Nadimpalli (2010) thesis — article abstract; thesis READ IN FULL
- Citation: Vamsi K. Nadimpalli & John J. Hasenbein, "When to challenge a call in tennis: A Markov decision process approach," *JQAS* 9(3): 229–238, 2013. DOI 10.1515/jqas-2012-0051. Thesis: V.K. Nadimpalli, "An average cost Markov decision process model to decide when to challenge a call in a tennis match," MS thesis, UT Austin, 2010 (https://repositories.lib.utexas.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e490d426-c6d3-43dd-9a26-22c76f977025/content).
- Model: a single game as a multi-chain average-reward MDP (absorbing win/lose states; solved by LP). State = (game score [20 states], last-point result, challenges remaining 0..q, player's perceived overturn probability at 4 levels s1..s4 = 0/25/50/75%, outcome type overturn/replay/second-serve); 164q+38 states. Parameters: p = point-win prob, r = replay prob after successful challenge, p_i = mix of perception levels. Objective = long-run average reward = P(win game).
- Findings: optimal policy is non-monotone in strength: "the player has to challenge if he is either too weak or too strong" (thesis p. 41); JQAS abstract: "some very weak or very strong players should not challenge" in some states; "multiple decision thresholds." No comparison to always/never-challenge (thesis); no empirical data beyond descriptive Tables 1.1–1.2: US Open 2009 men 360 challenges, 105 correct (29.8%), 6.32/match; Wimbledon 2010 men 260 challenges, 92 correct (35.4%), 5.65/match; "approximately 1.5% of the total points played were being challenged… only 30% resulted in overturned calls."
- Use for us: precedent for a discrete-belief MDP; the non-monotonicity in team strength is a hypothesis we can test with team-level WP (does the optimal ABS threshold vary with run differential/strength in a non-monotone way?). Left undone: no calibration to data, one game only, no tie-break, no opponent stock.

### 2.3 Clarke & Norman (2012) — abstract/metadata only (full text blocked)
- Citation: S.R. Clarke & J.M. Norman, "Optimal challenges in tennis," *Journal of the Operational Research Society* 63(12): 1765–1772, 2012. DOI 10.1057/jors.2011.147 (reprinted in *Operational Research Applied to Sports*, Palgrave 2015, pp. 224–239). Accepted manuscript at Swinburne figshare (https://figshare.swinburne.edu.au/articles/journal_contribution/Optimal_challenges_in_tennis/26217452; file 47522306).
- Abstract findings: DP over game/set with limited challenges; "players should be more aggressive in challenging in the latter stages of the games and sets, and when their opponent is ahead. Optimal challenge strategy can increase a player's chance of winning an otherwise even five-set match to 59%." Motivated explicitly by cricket and "similar challenge rules expected to be introduced in other sports."
- Use: the only tennis paper that reports a *value of the challenge right* under optimal play (a 9-pp swing in a 50-50 match is an upper-bound style number driven by their assumed error/notice rates — must not be quoted as empirical). Left undone: no data, no comparison to behavior.

### 2.4 Mather (2008) — abstract + secondary (full text blocked by NCBI/RS/EuropePMC)
- Citation: George Mather, "Perceptual uncertainty and line-call challenges in professional tennis," *Proc. R. Soc. B* 275(1643): 1645–1651, 2008. DOI 10.1098/rspb.2008.0211. PMC2602819.
- Data: 1,473 challenges by 246 professionals in 2006–2007 (TechCrunch summary of the paper). Method: psychophysical model—challenges as function of Hawk-Eye distance from line fit by a cumulative-Gaussian uncertainty model.
- Abstract findings: "the vast majority of challenges can be explained in a direct highly predictable manner by a simple model of uncertainty in perceptual information processing. Both players and line judges are remarkably accurate at judging ball bounce position, with a positional uncertainty of less than 40 mm. Line judges are more reliable than players. Judgements are more difficult for balls bouncing near base and service lines than those bouncing near side and centre lines. There is no evidence for significant errors in localization due to image motion." Judges "right 61 percent of the time when challenged."
- Use: template for our *perception model* (probability of challenge as a smooth function of true distance from the zone edge; role-specific SDs), and for the claim that a challenge system reveals perceptual noise. Left undone: no decision model; no stakes.

### 2.5 Whitney, Wurnitsch, Hontiveros & Louie (2008) — key results extracted
- Citation: D. Whitney, N. Wurnitsch, B. Hontiveros, E. Louie, "Perceptual mislocalization of bouncing balls by professional tennis referees," *Current Biology* 18(20): R947–R949, 2008.
- Data/finding: "Of a random set of 4,457 points drawn from the 2007 Wimbledon championship, referees made 83 erroneous judgments… the majority (84%) were errors in which the ball was reported as being shifted in the direction of the ball's motion" (70 predicted vs 13 unpredicted; χ²(1)=39.1, p<.0001). Among televised Hawk-Eye challenges with replays: 28 predicted vs 6 unpredicted errors. Advice: players "should concentrate their challenges on balls that are called 'out'"; alternatively "every shot… objectively reviewed."
- Use: directional bias of official errors → directional asymmetry in *which calls to challenge* (in ABS: called strikes off the edge vs called balls in the zone; count-dependent umpire bias per Green & Daniels). AEKM used Whitney's data to calibrate p_close = 2%.

### 2.6 Anbarci, Lee & Ulker (2016) — READ IN FULL
- Citation: Nejat Anbarci, Jungmin Lee, Aydogan Ulker, "Win at All Costs or Lose Gracefully in High-Stakes Competition? Gender Differences in Professional Tennis," *Journal of Sports Economics* 17(5): 536–560, 2016. DOI 10.1177/1527002514531788. Open PDF: https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1323073
- Data: ATP Masters and WTA Tier I events 2006–2008 (2-challenge era; Grand Slams excluded), tour challenge reports; 331 men's + 149 women's matches; 1,484 challenges (1,057 men, 427 women); 113 men, 91 women. Same threshold model as AEKM but adds shame cost c and pride b (Eq. 2); player-by-match fixed effects; FE-Tobit for utilization, FE-logit for success.
- Findings: utilization similar (0.314 vs 0.335 challenges per opportunity per set); success 39.8% men vs 32.5% women (n.s. overall). At tiebreaks women's success rises and men's falls (significant); "embarrassing challenges" (ball >50 mm out—about one ball diameter) are 17% overall but 34% for men vs 9% for women in tiebreaks; men's challenges correlate with opponents' (r=0.164, p=.003; women 0.052 n.s.); higher-ranked men make *more* embarrassing challenges, higher-ranked women fewer. Quote: "Low utilization rates suggest that there is a stigma associated with a wrong or unnecessary challenge, and/or that there is a strong incentive to save a challenge for a critical moment" (p. 14). Limitation (p. 10): cannot separate shame cost from overconfidence.
- Use: (a) defines a clean "low-quality challenge" metric (distance beyond a physical threshold) we can port to ABS (pitch center > 1 ball-radius outside the zone edge); (b) shows late-set/high-stakes "desperation" challenges — the tennis analogue of the 9th-inning dump; (c) tie-break provocation/contagion effects → test whether one team's challenge raises the other's hazard.

### 2.7 Kovalchik, Sackmann & Reid (2017) — abstract only
- Citation: Stephanie A. Kovalchik, Jeff Sackmann, Machar Reid, "Player, official or machine?: uses of the challenge system in professional tennis," *Int. J. Performance Analysis in Sport* 17(6): 961–969, 2017. DOI 10.1080/24748668.2017.1410340.
- Abstract (OpenAlex reconstruction): 1,037 challenged and 154,648 unchallenged shots at the 2016 Australian Open; regression + ML. Closer to lines players "were more likely to challenge fast shots (OR = 1.41, p < .001)"; for men, point importance (OR = 1.12, p = .003) and a previous successful challenge (OR = 1.84, p < .001) predict challenging; ball and player location most important for success; "Players frequently delayed challenges and there was evidence of [impact/loss] aversion among officials"; recommends reducing delayed challenges and chair-umpire bias.
- Use: the only tennis paper with the *unchallenged* denominator (like our per-pitch data); the "hot-hand after a successful challenge" and "delayed challenge" findings are direct hypotheses for ABS (timing rule = immediate tap; post-success confidence). Left undone: one tournament; no DP benchmark.

### 2.8 Almog, Gauriot, Page & Martin (2024; v3 Feb 2025) — READ IN FULL
- Citation: David Almog, Romain Gauriot, Lionel Page, Daniel Martin, "AI Oversight and Human Mistakes: Evidence from Centre Court," arXiv:2401.16754 (v3, 12 Feb 2025); extended abstract in *Proc. 25th ACM EC* 2024 (doi 10.1145/3670865.3673481).
- Data: Hawk-Eye tracking Mar 2005–Mar 2009 (1.8M bounces, 1,800+ men's matches) merged with ATP challenge records (2,784 challenges, 2006–08) and 43 video-audited matches; analysis sample 698 matches, 35 tournaments (109 pre-Hawk-Eye-review matches from 7 tournaments = broadcast-only period; 589 post), 99,337 points; 2,038 of 2,108 challenges merged (97%). Ground truth = Hawk-Eye (3.6 mm mean error).
- Design: before/after introduction of the *challenge* system holding umpire pool/training constant; LPM of incorrect-call on PostHK with 20-mm distance bins, speed, score, round, tier; SE clustered by match.
- Findings: baseline error 0.61% of all bounces, 13.9% within 100 mm, 32.9% within 20 mm. Post-review: −1.1 pp within 100 mm (≈ −8%, p<.05); non-serves −2.3 pp (−17%). But within 20 mm the share called "in" rose 42.8% → 49.0% (+6.2 pp, +12.6%); errors rose +8.6 pp for balls 20–40 mm *out* and fell −7.7 pp for balls 0–20 mm *in*; close serves: error +7.3 pp (+22.9%). Effect concentrated in early rounds (less-skilled crews) and grew over 12–18 months (Fig. 3–4; ≈0.45 pp/month). Structural rational-inattention model: attention costs κ_I=0.580, κ_O=0.510; psychological penalty of being overturned c_I=−1.374 (Type II) vs c_O=−0.903 (Type I) → "umpires cared 37% more about Type II errors under AI oversight." Mechanism: an overturned "out" call cannot be replayed cleanly, so it is more publicly costly.
- Quotes: "the particular implementation guidelines of the oversight system can shift the incentives for the decision-maker" (Sec. 6). "Umpires may be inclined to reduce the now costlier type of error… at the expense of increasing, by more than a 1:1 ratio, the now relatively less costly type of error." Notes Hawk-Eye/ABS in baseball and cites Cho (2023) on umpires with AI assistance but does not analyze baseball.
- Use: the direct template for our umpire-accountability pillar (Triple-A full-ABS vs challenge; 2025 spring trial; 2026 MLB): predict lower overall miss rate but a *directional* shift toward whichever error is less likely to be challenged/overturned publicly (e.g., fewer called strikes off the plate if batters challenge more than pitchers/catchers). Left undone: no team/role heterogeneity, no learning of challengers, no rule counterfactuals.

### 2.9 Bassetti, Bonini, Pacicco & Pavesi (2019 WP; SSRN "Definitivity Avoidance") — READ IN FULL (WP)
- Citation: Thomas Bassetti, Stefano Bonini, Fausto Pacicco, Filippo Pavesi, "Play it again! A Natural Experiment on Reversibility Bias," Marco Fanno WP 0238, U. Padova, 2019 (https://economia.unipd.it/sites/decon.unipd.it/files/20190238.pdf). Same authors' SSRN 3432981 "Definitivity Avoidance" (doi 10.2139/ssrn.3432981; Bonini's site lists it as "Definitivity Avoidance as a Source of Informational Inefficiency," working paper) appears to be the renamed version — SSRN was proxy-blocked, so treat the SSRN abstract as unverified.
- Design: line judges avoid *definitive* calls; introducing reversibility (challenges) should raise definitive calls. Outcome = ace ratio (aces are the definitive outcome of a non-call). Diff-in-diff across Grand Slams (US Open 2006, AO/Wimbledon 2007; Roland Garros placebo), Sackmann match data 2002–2010, 639–1,010 matches, pair FE.
- Findings: ace ratio +1.35 to +1.51 pp (2007–09), ≈2 corrected serves per match; +3.2–3.5 pp with pair-tournament FE; effect fades by 2010; clay placebo ≈ 0. Cites "6.85 challenges/match men, 4.14 women, only 27% overturned." Weak proxy design (aces confounded by serving trends) — cite cautiously.
- Use: second piece of evidence that review changes officials' *type* of decision; conceptual language ("reversibility") for why a challenge system may make umpires more willing to call borderline strikes/balls.

### 2.10 Carboch, Vejvodová & Süss (2016) — abstract
- "Analysis of errors made by line umpires on ATP tournaments," *IJPAS* 16(1), 2016. Two Masters-1000 events, official Hawk-Eye records via ATP: umpire error in 27% of reviewed calls; one error per 17.4 games; mean error size 33.2 mm; most at baseline; fewer/smaller errors late in tournament; argues for "1 error per N games" metrics rather than %.

### 2.11 Collins & Evans (2008; 2011) — abstracts
- H. Collins & R. Evans, "You cannot be serious! Public understanding of technology with special reference to 'Hawk-Eye'," *Public Understanding of Science* 17(3): 283–308, 2008; and "Sport-decision aids and the 'CSI-effect': Why cricket uses Hawk-Eye well and tennis uses it badly," *PUS* 21(8): 904–921, 2011. ### 2.12 Sackmann / Tennis Abstract blog — READ
- "How Much Is a Challenge Worth?" (Mar 9, 2016): ATP challenge ≈ once per 60 points (1.7%), ~30% overturned; Monte Carlo with 65% serve-point win: player exhausts challenges and then faces a wrong call ≈ once per 320 sets (0.31%); when it happens the missed challenge costs 12.4 pp of set-win probability (≈20 pp in 85-point sets); net ≈ one set lost per 2,600 matches. "Challenges by Gender" (Sep 1, 2013): 2013 Slam Hawk-Eye summaries, men 7.52 challenges/match vs women 3.38; per point, men every 32 points vs women every 37. "What Happens After an Unsuccessful First Serve Challenge?" (Feb 4, 2016): 116 ATP / 89 WTA cases; no post-challenge performance effect; men win 38% / women 32% of first-serve challenges.
- Forbes (Sep 4, 2009): 2008 US Open men 94/314 overturned (30%), women 54/152 (36%); 6.5 challenges per men's match; Federer 5-of-28 (18%), Murray 24%, Djokovic 30%, Nadal 36%, Blake 55%; Jankovic 6-of-22.
- Barnett, "Challenges with the tennis challenge system," *Significance* (Jun 17, 2011): argues the budget distorts play; proposes unlimited challenges on high-importance points. Pollard, Pollard, Barnett & Zeleznikow, "Applying strategies to the tennis challenge system," *J. Medicine & Science in Tennis* 15(1): 12–15, 2010: strategy discussion, no data.

### 2.13 Rules and timeline (verified sources)
- ITF Electronic Review procedures (as reproduced by tournaments, e.g., https://btopen.org/review/): "Each player is allowed three (3) unsuccessful appeals per set, plus one (1) additional appeal in the tie-break"; advantage sets restart with 3 at 6-6 and every 12 games; match tie-break starts with 3; "Players will have an unlimited number of successful appeals"; request only "on either a point-ending shot or when a player stops playing the point during a rally (returns are permitted but then the player must immediately stop)"; chair umpire "may refuse the Electronic Review if… unreasonable request or… not made in a timely manner"; if the system cannot decide, "the original call or overrule will always stand"; chair umpire's decision "is not appealable"; doubles: improper stop = hindrance, point lost.
- History: ITF/ATP/USTA approval Oct 2005; Hopman Cup Jan 2006; tour debut Miami Mar 22 2006 (Jamea Jackson first challenge, unsuccessful); US Open Aug 2006 (2 per set + 1 tiebreak); AO and Wimbledon 2007; March 2008 harmonization to 3 unsuccessful per set (+1 in tiebreak). Hawk-Eye Live: Next Gen ATP Finals 2017/18; US Open outer courts 2020; AO all courts 2021; US Open all courts 2022; ATP release Apr 28 2023 → all ATP events from 2025 incl. clay (Monte-Carlo, Madrid, Rome 2025); Wimbledon announced Oct 9 2024 → 2025 (with unlimited *video* review of non-line calls, ELC not challengeable); Roland Garros keeps human line judges in 2026 (Tennis Majors, May 7 2026). Under ELC Live the challenge decision problem disappears entirely.

## 4. Claims we must NOT overclaim / traps
- "Tennis players are near-optimal" is AEKM's *value-captured* metric (97%), not threshold accuracy; they simultaneously find systematic under-challenging and 6–8 pp lower success on high-stakes points. Do not say tennis players "solve the DP."
- AEKM's rule was 2 challenges per set (2006–08); the current tennis rule (3) postdates their data. Our 2-token/game ABS rule is closer to 2006-tennis than to 2008+ tennis; say so.
- Success-rate-as-threshold-proxy needs Assumption 1 (belief distribution invariant to state). With per-pitch ground truth we can *test* it rather than assume it — say we improve on AEKM, not that they were wrong.
- Whitney's 84% is *of referee errors on close balls at Wimbledon 2007*, not of all calls; Mather's "61%" is share of challenges that were unsuccessful, not umpire accuracy overall (umpire error is ~0.6% of bounces, ~14% within 100 mm; Almog et al.).
- Clarke & Norman's 59% and Sackmann's "one set per 2,600 matches" are model/simulation numbers under assumed error rates, not observed effects.
- Almog et al. identify a *crew-level* effect (chair + line judges) before/after 2006 in men's ATP; the "AI oversight" framing is theirs — for ABS the review is player-triggered and the ground truth is the same Hawk-Eye system, so accountability is closer, not identical.
- Bassetti et al.'s ace-ratio design is indirect; SSRN "Definitivity Avoidance" abstract not verified — cite the Padova WP by name if used.
- Kovalchik et al. abstract reconstructed from OpenAlex; "impact/loss aversion among officials" wording uncertain — verify before quoting.
- Nadimpalli & Hasenbein: only abstract read; details above come from the 2010 thesis (single-game model), which the JQAS version may extend.
- Do not claim tennis has public challenge datasets: it does not (see §6). Do not claim Sackmann's point-by-point files carry challenge flags: they do not.

## 5. Open questions we can answer that tennis could not
- Revealed thresholds by *role* (batter vs catcher vs pitcher) and by *team* budget-holder — impossible in tennis (single decision-maker); the agency problem AEKM invoke against Romer becomes testable.
- Threshold with the unchallenged denominator on every pitch (Kovalchik had one tournament); direct test of Assumption 1 (belief distribution vs. state), of the "9th-inning dump," and of learning within a season (AEKM had no learning test; Almog found umpires adapt over 12–18 months).
- Directional accountability: does the challenge system push umpires toward the *less-challenged* error type (called strikes on borderline pitches vs called balls), replicating Almog's Type I/II shift, and does it differ under full ABS (no accountability, no discretion) vs challenge ABS?
- Perceptual model by role (Mather-style SDs; ~40 mm in tennis) with 3D pitch location, batter height zone, catcher framing incentives — a catcher-framing/challenge equilibrium has no tennis analogue.
- Rule counterfactuals with real behavior (1/2/3 tokens; retention on success; who may challenge; extra-inning token) — tennis only did this in DP toys (Clarke & Norman) and simulations (Sackmann); MLB's 2025 spring/2026 rollout gives real variation.
- Contagion/provocation (Anbarci r=0.16 for men) and hot-hand-after-success (Kovalchik OR=1.84) at the pitch level.

## 6. Public data sources found (tennis)
- No public per-challenge dataset exists. Academic samples were private (ATP umpire sheets 2006–08 for AEKM/Anbarci; Hawk-Eye tracking for Almog; ATP records for Carboch; AO 2016 for Kovalchik). Tour/Slam websites published only per-match challenge summaries (Slamtracker era), which Sackmann hand-collected in 2013.
- Jeff Sackmann GitHub (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): tennis_slam_pointbypoint (Grand Slam point-by-point since 2011; columns incl. Speed_KMH, RallyCount, ServeWidth/Depth, ReturnDepth — **no challenge/Hawk-Eye field**); tennis_pointbypoint (~74k matches, sequences of S/R/A/D — no challenge field); tennis_MatchChartingProject (shot-by-shot; no challenge codes). Useful only for point-importance/win-probability calibration (serve-point win rates) as in Sackmann 2016.
- Aggregate numbers usable for a "tennis vs ABS" table: challenge rate 1.5–2.6% of points; success 27–38%; 5.4–7.5 challenges per men's match, 3.4–4.1 per women's; run-out ≈0.3% of sets (simulated); umpire error 0.6% of bounces / 13.9% within 100 mm / 32.9% within 20 mm (Almog); Hawk-Eye error 3.6 mm (ITF limit 5 mm).
- Rule text: ITF/Grand Slam electronic review procedures (reproduced at https://btopen.org/review/); ATP Official Rulebook PDFs (atptour.com/-/media/files/rulebook/…); ATP ELC release Apr 28 2023 (https://www.atptour.com/en/news/electronic-line-calling-release-april-2023).
- Cross-domain pointer for the ABS readers: baseballsavant.mlb.com/abs (ABS Challenge Dashboard) surfaced in search; KBO ABS papers (arXiv 2407.15779 / Sci. Rep. 2025; Eur. Sport Mgmt Q. 2026 "Technology adoption and bias in officiating") — not read here.
