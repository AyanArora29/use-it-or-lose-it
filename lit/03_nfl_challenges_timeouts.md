# Domain 03 — NFL replay challenges & timeouts as scarce resources; NBA/NFL timeouts; use-it-or-lose-it / end-of-period dumping

Reader notes for the SSAC27 ABS-challenge paper. Date: 2026-08-17. Web-fetch only (paywalled sites ESPN/Sportico/BP blocked); nflverse pbp 2013–2025 was downloaded and analyzed directly (scripts + CSVs in `/home/claude/sloan/lit/scratch/`).

## 1. Domain summary

There is surprisingly little *peer-reviewed* work on the NFL coach's challenge itself; the best analyses are two practitioner models — Krasker (2006), a full backward-induction DP of challenge value with a renewable-token structure (third challenge if both succeed, timeout restored on success), and Burke (2014), a break-even formula B = (N−U)/(R−U) showing coaches need only a 3–12% reversal probability to justify a challenge because a timeout is cheap outside the last minutes. Both conclude coaches are, if anything, too *conservative* — echoing Romer's (2006, JPE) canonical fourth-down finding that NFL teams systematically depart from win-maximization. The NBA coach's challenge (2019–, one token; second token if the first succeeds since 2023–24; timeout retained on success, lost on failure) is the closest structural analog to ABS and shows a clean end-of-period pattern: 51% of challenges come in Q4 and success rates fall monotonically by quarter (65/53/45/39% in 2019-20; >60% in Q1–3 vs 49% in Q4 in 2024-25). MLB's own 2014–2025 manager-challenge system (one token, second if the first succeeds) shows the same inning gradient (58.5% → 49.6% → 38.8% overturn by thirds of the game; Imber 2015), and two Hardball Times DP-style pieces (Wolfersberger 2014; Illarionov 2017) already argued managers should challenge at ~20–50% success probability rather than "save" the flag. Timeout-valuation work (Krasker 2005; Burke 2014; Soltoff 2016) shows the clock-management value of an NFL timeout is small (≈0.005–0.01 WP mid-game, up to 0.03 trailing late) and confounded in observational data. The economics of expiring budgets (Liebman & Mahoney 2017, AER: last-week federal spending is 4.9× a normal week and 2–6× more likely to be low quality) provides the "use-it-or-lose-it" theory: precautionary hoarding under uncertain future needs plus a hard expiry produces a low-quality end-of-period dump. Our nflverse pull (2013–2025) confirms the NFL versions of these facts: ~0.30 challenges per team-game, 72% of team-games with zero challenges, reversal rate ≈45%, Q1 51% vs Q4 42%, lowest (35%) in the last challengeable 3 minutes, and 39% of Q4 challenges thrown when the challenger's WP is <5% or >95%.

## 2. Per-work entries

### 2.1 Krasker, William S. (2006). "A Model for Coaches' Challenges." footballcommentary.com, Mar 6 2006. http://www.footballcommentary.com/challenges.htm
- **Data**: 71 NFL games (3,683 minutes) hand-coded for "reversible rulings": 110 found = 0.84 challenge opportunities per team per game; "nearly a third of the reversible rulings involved a change of possession." Reversal-probability histogram is bimodal ("relatively few challenge opportunities with success probabilities near 0.5").
- **Method**: Backward-induction DP in MATLAB. State: time, score, timeouts, challenges used, whether a challenge has failed. Assumes 24 alternating 2:30 possessions; one challenge opportunity may arise per possession. Rules encoded: "If both of a team's challenges are successful, the team is awarded a third challenge, but a fourth challenge is never permitted"; "A coach's challenge requires the use of a team timeout. However, if the challenge is successful, the timeout is restored." Timeout value calibrated to his 2005 clock-management model (~0.008–0.01 WP).
- **Findings**: Value of holding two challenges vs one "is only 0.002 even at the start of the game." Value of the *last* challenge (Table 1; two timeouts left, one failed challenge used): tie game 0.0059 (15:00 left) → 0.0107 (60:00 left); ±14 points 0.0008–0.0066. Worked examples: NO should have challenged (benefit 0.05 vs cost ≈0.0055); DEN correctly saved a 10%-reversal challenge (0.0043 vs 0.0135); NE was right to challenge a 10% chance when the WP swing was 0.3.
- **Quotable**: "a coach with two challenges left should challenge even a relatively inconsequential ruling, provided the probability of reversing the ruling is high." / "The decision to challenge must take into account the likelihood that the challenge will succeed, and the cost of losing a timeout if the challenge fails."
- **Relation**: The direct ancestor of our DP (finite horizon, renewable token, opportunity arrivals, bimodal p(reversal)). Undone: no empirical revealed-threshold estimation, tiny sample, no learning, single-decision-maker.

### 2.2 Krasker, W. S. (2005). "The Clock-Management Value of Timeouts." footballcommentary.com, Jun 14 2005. https://www.footballcommentary.com/timeouts.htm
- DP over 2nd-half states (time, score, timeouts each side, possession); no field position. Incremental value of a 2nd vs 1st timeout: tied 0.0057–0.0075 WP; trailing by 1 with 5:00 left 0.0353; leading by 8 ≈0.0003–0.0023. "The win probability is approximately linear in the number of timeouts remaining." Use: gives the *cost* side of the NFL challenge; ABS has no analogous side-cost (the token itself is the only cost), which is why our DP is cleaner than the NFL case.

### 2.3 Burke, Brian (2014). "Analyzing Replay Challenges." Advanced Football Analytics, Sep 8 2014. http://www.advancedfootballanalytics.com/2014/09/analyzing-replay-challenges.html
- **Method**: Break-even reversal probability B = (N−U)/(R−U), N = WP no challenge, R = WP if reversed, U = WP if upheld (timeout lost). Six Week-1 challenges: break-evens 3–12%.
- **Findings/quotes**: "coaches are not blowing their timeouts on frivolous challenges"; challenge "if a call is fishy at all—say somewhere in the neighborhood of a 1-in-10 or 1-in-20 chance of being overturned"; timeouts matter "late in close games," otherwise "there are just too many different and more important factors than timeouts." Explicitly sets aside the last-timeout / third-challenge cases as "exceptionally rare."
- **Relation**: The one-line threshold rule we generalize (our threshold has an option-value term because the token is scarce; his has only the timeout cost).

### 2.4 Burke, B. (2014). "The Value of a Timeout — A First Approximation" (Jan 21) and "Part 2" (Jan 22). AFA. http://www.advancedfootballanalytics.com/2014/01/the-value-of-timeout-first-approximation.html ; .../the-value-of-timeout-part-2.html
- Cross-sectional WP by timeouts remaining (3Q, 1-score lead, midfield; largest cell n=3,063): first pass ≈0.05 WP; logit refinement ≈0.027–0.031 WP per timeout (offense coef +0.141, p<.001; defense −0.111, p=.07). Burke flags the confound: teams "in trouble" have already spent timeouts. Soltoff (2016, https://bensoltoff.com/blog/valuing-timeouts-in-football/) replicated on Armchair Analysis 2002–2014 (n=3,560) and found *no* significant timeout effect (offense −0.04, defense −0.14, CIs overlapping). Lesson for us: revealed value of a token in observational data is selection-contaminated; use the DP/structural value, not naïve WP-by-tokens-remaining regressions.

### 2.5 Romer, David (2006). "Do Firms Maximize? Evidence from Professional Football." *Journal of Political Economy* 114(2): 340–365. https://eml.berkeley.edu/~dromer/papers/PAPER_NFL_JULY05_FORWEB_CORRECTED.pdf
- **Data**: 1998–2000 regular seasons (732 games); first-quarter plays to estimate values (11,112 situations; 2,560 kicks; 4,733 third downs); DP for value of field position.
- **Findings**: On the 1,068 fourth downs where going for it is better, teams kicked 959 times; on 1,604 where kicking is better, they went 9 times. Cost ≈2.1 pct-points of win probability per game-equivalent, "roughly one win every three seasons."
- **Quotes**: choices "differ from the choices that would maximize their chances of winning in ways that are systematic and overwhelmingly statistically significant" (p.2); "individuals may want to maximize the chances of winning, but do so imperfectly...rely on experience and intuition rather than formal analysis" (p.30).
- **Relation**: The canonical "professionals deviate from optimal" template (and the model for how to present optimal-vs-observed tables). Our contribution mirrors it for a review right.

### 2.6 Sandholtz, N., Wu, L., Puterman, M. & Chan, T. C. Y. (2023/24). "Learning Risk Preferences in Markov Decision Processes: An Application to the Fourth Down Decision in the NFL." arXiv:2309.00756. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00756
- nflfastR 2014–2022; inverse optimization: assume observed decisions are optimal under an unknown quantile-risk criterion; estimate τ minimizing Hamming distance between observed and quantile-optimal policies. Coaches optimize low quantiles (τ≈0.3–0.5 own half; 0.4–0.6 opponent half); τ rose over 2014–2022; higher τ associates with more points (β=0.769). Quote: "we find that coaches' fourth-down behavior is consistent with optimizing low quantiles of the next-state value distribution, which corresponds to conservative risk preferences."
- **Relation**: Methodological template for "revealed threshold vs optimal" (our players' revealed challenge thresholds could be reported as an implied risk/misperception parameter rather than raw deviations).

### 2.7 Liebman, Jeffrey B. & Mahoney, Neale (2017). "Do Expiring Budgets Lead to Wasteful Year-End Spending? Evidence from Federal Procurement." *AER* 107(11): 3510–49. DOI 10.1257/aer.20131296 (NBER w19481).
- 14.6M federal contracts 2004–2009. Last-week spending is 4.9× a typical week (8.7% of annual spending in week 52 vs 1.9% in a typical week). Year-end IT projects "between two and six times more likely to have a lower quality rating." DOJ rollover authority natural experiment: IT (rollover) last-week share 3.4% vs non-IT 9.3%. Model: uncertain needs + expiring budget → precautionary saving → low-quality dump; rollover would fix it (Congress could give "87 cents on the dollar" and get the same value).
- **Relation**: The theory and vocabulary for the "9th-inning dump": tokens that expire at game end + uncertainty about future close pitches ⇒ rational hoarding early and a lower-quality burst late. Retention-on-success is a partial "rollover." Trap: their dump is *wasteful*; a late-game challenge dump can be *optimal* (token has zero continuation value), so we must separate optimal late use from suboptimal early hoarding.

### 2.8 Imber, Lindsay (2015). "Reviewing Instant Replay: Observations and Implications from Replay's Inaugural Season." *SABR Baseball Research Journal* 44(1). https://sabr.org/journal/article/reviewing-instant-replay-observations-and-implications-from-replays-inaugural-season/
- 2014 MLB: 1,274 reviews in 2,428 games; 1,053 manager challenges + 221 crew-chief reviews; 47.5% overturned. Overturn by game phase: innings 1–3 58.5%, 4–6 49.6%, 7+ 38.8% (caution: 7+ includes crew-chief reviews, which overturn only 23%). Catch/trap 76% overturned; Rule 7.13 14%. Low-volume challengers (Girardi .793, Redmond .731) had the highest success — selective use. Quote: "Managers would be wise to challenge or request an otherwise benign review when on defense, during stressful situations."
- **Relation**: Baseball's first renewable-token review right; the inning gradient is the pre-ABS baseline for the "dump."

### 2.9 Wolfersberger, Jesse (2014). "When Should Managers Challenge?" *The Hardball Times*, Feb 14 2014. https://tht.fangraphs.com/when-should-managers-challenge/
- 2013 Retrosheet pbp (130k challenge-eligible plays; 54.2/game); assumes ~1% umpire error; expected 0.27 obviously-challengeable calls per game, 0.05 at LI≥1.5. Opportunity-cost model: challenge when WPA×p(reverse) exceeds the expected value of foregone future challenges, which falls through the game. Conclusion: "If there is a call that you have even a 50/50 chance of reversing, you should probably challenge, no matter the inning."

### 2.10 Illarionov, Kolya (2017). "Should Managers Save Their Challenges?" *The Hardball Times*, May 30 2017. https://tht.fangraphs.com/should-managers-save-their-challenges/
- 4,090 challenges in 14,574 games (2014–16), 49.6% overturned (40.4% late innings). Builds "LI-7" (leverage relative to remaining challengeable innings) and expected future overturns (0.11 per team-game, declining linearly to zero by the 7th). Average value of a successful challenge ≈0.65 runs (≈6.5% WE). Threshold: challenge whenever p(success) ≳ 20%; "a manager's optimal strategy is to challenge as much as he can."
- **Relation**: Closest baseball precedent to our DP threshold; undone: no per-manager revealed thresholds, no formal DP, no perception model, pre-Statcast.

### 2.11 NBA coach's challenge (rule + data). Rule No. 14 (https://official.nba.com/rule-no-14-coachs-challenge/): "Each team is entitled to one Challenge per game. If a team's first Challenge is successful, the team will be awarded a second Challenge"; must "call a legal timeout immediately"; success → "retains its timeout"; failure → "loses its timeout"; only own fouls, out-of-bounds, goaltending (no goaltending challenges in last 2:00). Data: Sprung, Forbes Jan 24 2020 (378 challenges through Jan 20 2020): 44% overturned; fouls 84% of challenges at 39%, OOB 76%, goaltending 64%; **share by quarter Q1 7%/Q2 16%/Q3 26%/Q4 51% with overturn 65/53/45/39%**; quotes: Quin Snyder "You wanna hang on to your challenges because you might need it later"; Joe Borgia "I'm not sure anybody thought we would be at 44%, 45% correct." NBA official (Dec 2020): 2019-20 REG 633 challenges, 44%; playoffs 67, 40%. Axios/NBA 2023-24: 1,282 challenges, 59.2% (CHI 77.3%, GSW 31.8%). Blazer's Edge (Jan 5 2025): 734 challenges, 58.4%; OOB 73.4%, fouls 51.4%; **>60% in Q1–3 vs 49% in Q4 (41% on fouls)**. Paraball Notes (Parasar 2024): EV per successful challenge 0.83–1.25 pts vs option value of holding 0.91 (one token) / 1.24 (two tokens); recommends aggressiveness late.
- **Relation**: The structurally closest live system (1 token, +1 on success, timeout retained/lost). Doubling of usage and +15 pts success after the 2023-24 second-token rule is a retention-rule natural experiment (confounded by learning/replay tech). Undone academically: nobody has estimated a DP or revealed thresholds for NBA challenges — a cheap cross-sport robustness exercise for us.

### 2.12 NFL rules & recent facts (for related-work accuracy). NFL Ops history (https://operations.nfl.com/the-game/history-of-instant-replay/): 1986–91 booth system, 12.6% reversal; 1999 coaches' challenge (two per game, timeout charged only if unsuccessful); 2004 third challenge if both succeed; 2007 permanent; 2011 all scoring plays booth-reviewed, 2012 turnovers (Wikipedia); 2014 NY consultation; 2019 PI reviewable one year; 2021 replay assist (2021: 254 stoppages prevented, 127 would-be challenges — PFT Feb 2 2023); 1999–2024: 8,698 reviews in 6,695 games, 42% reversed, 61% in 2024, review time 2:33 → 1:25. **Mar 25 2024** (PFT): third challenge if *either* of the first two succeeds (Lions proposal). **2019 PI experiment**: 81–82 coach PI challenges, 13 reversed (16%) (SI 2020; nflverse confirms 82/13); PFT Nov 6 2019: 5/53 through week 9. **2025**: coaches now see the Hawk-Eye/replay-official feed; challenge success 60% through 7 weeks vs 40% in 2024 (Kahler, ESPN/TSN Oct 22 2025); a club official: "There are a couple of challenges the Hawk-Eye view has helped prevent me from throwing." **Aug 2026**: coaches may name a pregame "challenge designee" to throw the flag (SI Aug 10 2026: 151 challenges in 2025, most since 2019; "Some coaches have acknowledged challenging calls for emotional rather than strategic reasons").
- **Relation**: (i) "who may challenge" and "information available to the challenger" are live design levers in the NFL too; (ii) success rate jumped when challengers got the reviewer's feed — analogous to our role-level perception pillar and to any scoreboard/dugout feed in ABS.

### 2.13 Gibbs, C., Elmore, R. & Fosdick, B. (2022). "The causal effect of a timeout at stopping an opposing run in the NBA." *Annals of Applied Statistics* 16(3), DOI 10.1214/21-AOAS1545 (arXiv:2011.11691). 2017-18/2018-19; 4,684 matched runs (834 with timeouts); propensity matching; ATT −0.35 points (slightly harmful); "it is slightly disadvantageous to call a timeout during a run." Also Saavedra, Mukherjee & Bagrow (2012) *Sci. Rep.* 2:676 (timeouts play "a minor role"); Beasley, Greenwald & Agha (2013), Sport Management (USF): using a timeout on a final drive *adds* 22 s to reach FG range, having one saves 19 s. NBA 2017-18 rule (pr.nba.com Jul 12 2017): 14 timeouts, max 2 after the 3:00 mark of Q4 — a use-it-or-lose-it cliff. Ringer (Ruiz, Jan 12 2023): Burke's ≈3.1 pp per timeout figure; Michael Lopez (NFL) tracked "operational" timeouts 2018–21 as wasteful.
- **Relation**: Timeouts are the classic scarce in-game resource; the literature is mostly about *effects*, not *optimal timing*. Useful as a caution that "resource has small measured value" ≠ "resource is misused."

## 4. Claims we must NOT overclaim / traps
- **A late-game "dump" is a prediction of the optimal policy, not evidence of irrationality.** In every token system the continuation value → 0 at the horizon, so thresholds *should* fall (Krasker; THT pieces). The behavioral test is (a) *hoarding* — tokens expiring unused when positive-EV opportunities were passed earlier — and (b) dumping *before* the option value is gone. Liebman-Mahoney's dump is wasteful only because quality is observable and rollover is feasible.
- **Success-rate gradients by period are confounded** by (i) selection of call types (NBA fouls vs OOB; MLB crew-chief reviews after the 6th), (ii) plays' leverage, (iii) information (Hawk-Eye feed 2025; NBA replay tech), (iv) fatigue/perception. Do not read "success falls late" as "threshold falls late" without a perception model.
- **Timeout value estimates from observational WP regressions are selection-biased** (Burke vs Soltoff). Do not import "≈3 pp per timeout" as a fact.
- **NFL rules facts**: two challenges per game (not per half); third challenge required *both* to succeed 2004–2023, *either* since Mar 2024; scoring plays booth-reviewed since 2011, turnovers 2012; no coach challenges after the two-minute warning; failed challenge costs a timeout, success restores it. In nflverse there is no challenge-team column; parse it from `desc`; `posteam_timeouts_remaining` on the challenge row is post-charge.
- **NBA**: 44% (2019-20) → 59% (2023-24) is not purely learning; the second-token rule and tech changed simultaneously.
- Little of this literature is peer-reviewed; label practitioner sources as such.

## 5. Open questions we could answer
- No published paper estimates a DP-optimal policy *and* revealed thresholds for any renewable-token challenge system in a team sport (Krasker had 71 games; NBA/MLB pieces are descriptive). Our ABS DP + revealed thresholds fills this; the NBA (1 token, +1 on success) and NFL (nflverse) are natural cross-sport replications with identical code.
- Does the token constraint bind? In the NFL it barely does (0.3 uses/team-game; 3% of team-games use two); ABS has hundreds of called pitches per game, so scarcity is real — quantify "opportunities per token" across sports.
- Retention-rule counterfactuals have live natural experiments: NBA 2023-24 second token; NFL 2024 "either" rule; MLB 2014 vs later crew-chief thresholds — usable as validation of our rule-design simulator's directional predictions.
- Information/perception: NFL 2025 feed access raised success 40→60%; who challenges (coach vs designee, catcher vs batter) and what they see is a design lever we can model.
- Learning: 2019 PI challenges stayed at 3–9 per week all season despite a 16% success rate — evidence that within-season learning can be slow even for coaches with staffs; test whether ABS players learn faster (many more reps).
