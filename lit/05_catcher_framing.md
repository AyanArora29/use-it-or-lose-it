# Domain 05 — Catcher framing: measurement, value, decline, and framing under automated zones

_Reader notes for SSAC27 ABS-challenge paper. Compiled 2026-08-17. Web-search budget hit mid-task; Baseball Prospectus (JS wall) and The Athletic were unreadable — BP methods are reconstructed from Deshpande & Wyner's and SABR's descriptions._

## 1. Domain summary

Framing measurement went through three generations: (i) 2008–2011 "strike-zone-map" comparisons (Turkenkopf 2008; Fast 2011; Marchi 2011) that already implied 15–40 run seasonal spreads; (ii) 2015–2017 mixed/hierarchical models that jointly credit catcher, pitcher, batter and umpire (Judge–Pavlidis–Brooks 2015 at BP; Rosales & Spratt 2015 at SSAC; Deshpande & Wyner 2017 in JQAS), which shrank the best-catcher value to roughly +15 to +25 runs with wide credible intervals; and (iii) Statcast's shadow-zone framework (2019→; 0.125 runs/strike, pitcher+park adjusted, 2018+ only). Every serious system agrees on two facts we can lean on: a called strike is worth ~0.11–0.14 runs on average but 0.06 (0-0) to 0.54 (3-2) by count, and the catcher spread has compressed sharply — best-minus-worst ≈ 98 runs (2008, FanGraphs FRM), ≈ 33 (2014, SIS SZ±), ≈ 28–38 (Statcast 2019/2025), and about 18 runs through mid-August 2026 — as teams selected/taught framing and umpires got more accurate (and, in 2025, were graded on a 0.75-inch instead of 2-inch buffer). Under the 2026 challenge system, MLB's own accounting keeps framing pegged to the umpire's *initial* call and books overturns as a separate "challenge skill"; the early public evidence (Triple-A 2025, spring 2026, April 2026) says framing skill is roughly uncorrelated with challenge accuracy, that only ~19% of umpire-stolen strikes get challenged, and that framing runs are shrinking but not vanishing. Nobody has yet modeled framing and challenging as a joint equilibrium — that is our opening.

## 2. Per-work entries

### 2.1 Turkenkopf, D. (2008). "Framing the Debate." Beyond the Box Score (Apr 2008). https://www.beyondtheboxscore.com/2008/4/5/389840/framing-the-debate
- Data: 2007 PITCHf/x; called-strike rate by location vs. John Walsh's empirical zone (≥50% strike). Method: Strikes Above Average per 150 pitches × 0.161 runs/strike.
- Numbers: Zaun +5.30 SAA/150 (+0.85 runs/150), Laird −7.76 (−1.25). Author's own doubt: "the results seem wrong to me, but I can't figure out where the issue lies"; "the difference between Gregg Zaun and Gerald Laird is over 250 runs or 25 wins."
- Use: origin story + the earliest run-per-strike figure (0.161; Lindbergh quotes 0.13). Trap: raw comparisons with no controls overstate spread massively.

### 2.2 Marchi, M. (2011). "Evaluating catchers: framing pitches" (parts 1–3). The Hardball Times, Jul 8 2011. https://tht.fangraphs.com/evaluating-catchers-framing-pitches-part-3/
- Data: PITCHf/x 2008–May 2011. Method: model with umpire, batter, pitcher, catcher effects; strike probability by location and count; catcher effect applied per pitch with **count-specific run values** (first to do so).
- Numbers: 2010 leaders J. Molina 16.9, Lucroy 16.9, McCann 15.9; Posada −14.5. Quote: "A catcher can contribute more than one win (probably even two wins) just by his ability to frame pitches."

### 2.3 Fast, M. (2011). "Spinning Yarn: Removing the Mask." Baseball Prospectus, Sept 24 2011 (not directly readable) — as reported in Lindbergh, B. (2013), "The Art of Pitch Framing," Grantland. https://grantland.com/features/studying-art-pitch-framing-catchers-such-francisco-cervelli-chris-stewart-jose-molina-others/
- Fast: framing consistent year-to-year (more so than OBP/SLG); Molina saved 111 runs 2008–13 (0.50 runs/100 pitches), Doumit cost 155 (−0.55/100); Molina paid ~$1.5M/yr. Lindbergh already flags a "framing bubble" and: "if the human element goes, replaced by robo umps, then framing will go with it." Astros hired Fast; Cashman quote on Cervelli/Stewart ("Tremendous pitch framers").
- Use: market-adoption narrative (framing as an underpriced skill that got competed away — the same dynamic Baumann 2026 predicts for challenge skill).

### 2.4 Judge, J., Pavlidis, H. & Brooks, D. (2015). "Moving Beyond WOWY: A Mixed Approach to Measuring Catcher Framing." Baseball Prospectus, Feb 5 2015 (art. 25514); companion Pavlidis & Brooks, "Framing and Blocking Pitches: A Regressed, Probabilistic Model" (BP, 2014). SABR summary: https://sabr.org/latest/baseball-prospectus-a-mixed-approach-to-measuring-catcher-framing/
- Method (as described by SABR/D&W): probabilistic strike model from location, then a **mixed model** with catcher, pitcher, batter, umpire (and PITCHf/x) effects → CSAA (Called Strikes Above Average); 0.14 runs/strike (D&W note it includes swinging/foul strikes); "Retro" model back to 1988. D&W's critique: BP (and Marchi) "unrealistically assume umpires differ only in some base-rate of calling strikes."
- Later: Judge (2018) "Bayesian Bagging to Generate Uncertainty Intervals: A Catcher Framing Story," BP art. 38289 (uncertainty intervals). Unread (paywall/JS); cite via D&W (r = 0.94 with D&W; FanGraphs FRM r = 0.96 with BP).

### 2.5 Rosales, J. & Spratt, S. (2015). "Who Is Responsible For A Called Strike?" MIT SSAC 2015 (research-paper finalist), Baseball Info Solutions. PDF: https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/68d6be744d7efccc2207f571/68d6be744d7efccc22080619_1036_rppaper_Sloan%20Analytics%202015%20-%20Who%20Is%20Responsible%20For%20A%20Called%20Strike.pdf  [READ IN FULL]
- Data: BIS proprietary pitch charting 2010–2013 (method), 2014 (results); includes catcher **target/glove location** ("command" = distance from target).
- Method: Strike Zone Plus/Minus (SZ±): ~1-inch location grid baseline, then buckets for count (3-0: +5.6 pp; 0-1: −3.2 pp), command (<3.8 in from target +2.8 pp; >10.4 in −8.6 pp), batter handedness/plate half (±2–4 pp); an **iterative equal-split attribution** across catcher/pitcher/batter/umpire (~10 iterations to convergence), then regression by adding 250 league-average pitches. Run value: "The average difference in run expectancy between a ball and a strike amounts to .1189 runs."
- Numbers: 2014 SZRS Zunino 16, Conger 16, Montero 15, Lucroy 14; Navarro −17, Saltalamacchia −16 → spread ≈ 33 runs. Umpire top Kulpa +12; pitcher top Iwakuma +4; batter top M. Cabrera +3. Even/odd-year reliability: catchers 0.86, umpires 0.77, batters 0.50, pitchers 0.46. Independence check: pitcher SZ± across different catchers r = 0.80–0.91. Self-described as "more conservative" (16 vs ~24 runs for BP/Carruth): "even by our measurements catchers are undervalued in baseball's free agent market" (16 runs ≈ $11–12M).
- Quote: "the catcher, the pitcher, the batter, and the umpire all have their own independent influence on whether the pitch is called a strike or a ball."
- Relation/gap: their four-actor decomposition is the template for our **role-level perception** pillar (who "knows" the zone). They never had a counterfactual truth per pitch; ABS gives one for challenged pitches. Undone: umpire effects under accountability, any strategic (challenge) layer, batter-height zone.

### 2.6 Deshpande, S.K. & Wyner, A.J. (2017). "A hierarchical Bayesian model of pitch framing." JQAS 13(3): 95–112. doi:10.1515/jqas-2017-0027; arXiv:1704.00823.  [READ IN FULL]
- Data: PITCHf/x 2011–2015; 2014 fit: 701,490 pitches, 355,293 taken, N = 308,388 "frameable" (within 1 ft of zone); 93 umpires, 101 catchers, 719 pitchers, 1,010 batters. Validation on 2015.
- Method: logit(P(strike)) = umpire-specific intercept + umpire-specific slope × historical-GAM log-odds of location (hGAM fit on 2011–13, by 4 handedness combos) + count (11 dummies) + catcher + pitcher + batter effects; 3-level hierarchy (umpire params ~ N(pop, τ²=0.25)); Stan; Model 3 (constant player/count effects) wins out of sample (MSE 0.069 vs 0.073 location-only; fully umpire-specific Model 5 overfits: in-sample 0.061, out-of-sample 0.074).
- Run value: ρ(count) = E[runs | ball] − E[runs | strike] on taken pitches, 2011–14: 0-0 0.062, 0-1 0.057, 0-2 0.098, 2-0 0.201, 3-0 0.305, 3-1 0.327, 3-2 0.540; **weighted mean 0.11** (vs Judge et al. 0.14; Turkenkopf 0.161).
- Numbers: 2014 runs saved: Montero 25.7 [15.6, 35.1], Zunino 22.7, Lucroy 19.6 [8.2, 30.5], Conger 19.3, Rivera 18.8; worst Saltalamacchia −24.0; spread ≈ 50 runs. Bias-corrected CAFE (integrates over the league pitch mix): Conger 16.2, Vazquez 14.3, Rivera 14.0; Telis −25.1. Catcher effects span ±3 log-odds ("18% → 82%") and are as large as count effects. Umpire example at 2-0: Hernandez 50% contour 4.37 ft² vs DiMuro 3.53 ft². Year-to-year CAFE correlation 0.70–0.71 (2012–14) falling to 0.58 (2014–15) — "marked uptick in awareness of framing… umpires may have responded by calling stricter zones." Correlation with BP r = 0.94. Dollar translation: Lucroy's CI ⇒ "$5.7M and $21.34M" (~$0.7M/run).
- Quotable: "With a single season's worth of data, we cannot discriminate between good framers with the same certainty that we separate good framers from bad framers." (§5)
- Relation/gap: the canonical peer-reviewed framing model; umpire-specific location slopes = umpire heterogeneity we can re-estimate under accountability. Undone: no context beyond count (no leverage/inning), no sequencing, assumes independence across calls (cites Chen et al. 2016 negative autocorrelation), no truth label per pitch — ABS overturns give us one.

### 2.9 Statcast catcher framing (Baseball Savant / MLB glossary) + ABS metrics documentation (2026). https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/leaderboard/catcher-framing ; https://www.mlb.com/glossary/statcast/catcher-framing ; https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/abs-metrics-documentation ; changelog https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/changelog/2026-02-19-new-strike-zone-images
- Method: shadow zone = "roughly one ball width inside and one ball wide outside of the zone" (attack zones 11–19, 8 regions); "Catcher Framing Runs converts a catcher's called strikes to runs saved on a .125 runs per strike basis," with park and pitcher adjustments; model "only applicable to data starting in the 2018 season"; qualifier 6 called shadow pitches per team game.
- **2026 accounting (key for us):** "First, the umpire's initial call is used to establish the run value of catcher framing. (Exactly like the pre-ABS world.) The difference between the original and final call is considered a separate 'challenge skill' run value." Worked example: −0.46 framing + 1.00 challenge = +0.54 strikes. Also defined: Challenge Opportunity (~50% of pitches), "Reasonable Pitch" (~5%: wrong call, or correct-but-within-3-inches with RE288 value ≥ +0.30, or ≥20% challenge probability), and a **constant-token breakeven**: "The breakeven point of a challenge is [0.2 / 0.2 + run value of situation]" (e.g., +0.30 situation ⇒ 40%). MLB is implicitly valuing a token at 0.2 runs regardless of state — our DP makes that value state-dependent.
- Zone-definition break: from 2026 the Savant zone is "measured at midpoint of plate and based on batter's height" (pre-2026: "front of plate and dependent on batter's stance") ⇒ 2026 shadow-zone rates are **not comparable** to 2025.

## 4. Claims we must NOT overclaim / traps
- **Framing did not "die" in 2026.** Public evidence: ~98–99% of calls are un-challenged; only ~19% of umpire-stolen strikes were challenged in April 2026 (Verducci); Statcast still books framing on the initial call. Say "compressed," show numbers, avoid "obsolete."
- **Comparability breaks:** (a) Savant zone redefined in 2026 (midpoint of plate, height-based) ⇒ do not compare 2026 shadow strike rates/framing runs to ≤2025 leaderboards; recompute from raw plate_x/plate_z with one zone definition. (b) Statcast framing model applies 2018+ only. (c) 2025 umpire buffer change confounds any pre/post-2026 framing comparison. (d) 2026 partial-season values must be pro-rated (or use per-pitch rates), and 2025's spread is inflated by one outlier (Bailey +25).
- **Run per strike is count-dependent** (0.06 at 0-0 vs 0.54 at 3-2, D&W). Never apply a flat 0.125 to challenge decisions; challenged pitches are heavily selected toward two-strike/three-ball counts (AAA batter success 34–36% there).
- **Framing vs challenge-skill run values are not on the same scale:** framing runs are vs. league average at 0.125/strike; Statcast "net runs" for challenges are RE288 counting stats (Baumann: "a counting stat, unlike framing"). Don't add or rank them naively.
- **Attribution:** with a single season, D&W show ranks among good framers are indistinguishable (95% rank intervals span ~20 places). Report intervals if we rank catchers.
- **Selection:** framing-vs-challenge correlations (Petriello ~0; Staph slightly negative) come from small/selected samples (spring, AAA); the deciding catcher chooses which calls to challenge, so success rates are endogenous.
- **Do not claim ABS "changed the umpire zone"** without separating the 2026 challenge accountability from the 2025 grading change and the pre-existing accuracy trend (Andrews: umpires improved every year until 2024).
- KBO/NPB full-ABS papers do not measure framing — cite only for zone geometry/adaptation.

## 5. Open questions we can answer
1. Joint framing–challenge equilibrium: does a stolen strike raise the batter's challenge probability, and by how much per inch/probability of being wrong? (Statcast exposes `exp_rate_challenges`, `n_challenges_against`, `rate_overturns_against` per catcher — merge with framing runs.)
2. Does framing "sell" the call to the batter (deterrence) — i.e., is P(challenge | wrong call) lower for elite framers? Verducci's 19% figure suggests low detection; test heterogeneity by catcher and by count/leverage.
3. Framing value in a challenge world = initial-call value × P(unchallenged) + P(challenged)×(−overturn cost) — compute an "ABS-adjusted framing run" and compare with Statcast's split.
4. Is framing skill uncorrelated with challenge accuracy at MLB scale (Petriello AAA finding), and are elite framers over-confident (Staph)? Selection-corrected role-level perception model.
