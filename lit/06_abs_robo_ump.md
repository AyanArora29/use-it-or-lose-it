## 1. Domain summary

The public record on ABS splits into (a) one pre-ABS "impact of robot umpires" paper (Zhan, Gerstner & Polimeni, SSAC 2020) built on a fixed zone; (b) MLB's own rule texts and trial statistics (Triple-A 2022–25, spring 2025, MLB 2026) that describe a *renewable review right* (2 tokens, retained if correct, +1 per extra inning if none) with a legislated 2-D zone (17 in wide, 27%–53.5% of measured height, mid-plate plane, any-part-of-ball); (c) 2026 analytics journalism (FanGraphs: Clemens, Jaffe, Andrews, Martell, Oler, Baumann) that already documents the key stylized facts we intend to model — success rates by role (C ≈ 58–59%, B ≈ 48%, P ≈ 37–41%), the ninth-inning "dump" (success 35% with two tokens in hand in the 9th vs ~59% in the 1st), higher challenge rates and lower success in high-leverage counts (3-2: 9.4% challenge rate, 40% success), a 2–5% shrinkage of the *called* zone in 2026 and a ~+1 pt walk-rate rise; (d) KBO full-ABS papers (Lee, Han & Ko 2025 Sci Rep; Hwang, Kim & Lee 2026 IMechE-P; Song, Kang & Paulsen 2026 ESMQ) on how a machine zone differs from a human one; and (e) a Cornell FAccT 2026 ethnography on ABS as technological rule enforcement. Crucially, *no* published work solves the challenge decision as a finite-horizon dynamic program with a renewable token, estimates revealed thresholds against it, or corrects role-level success rates for selection; Baseball Savant's own "Confidence Level" uses a *constant* 0.2-run token cost (breakeven p = 0.2/(0.2+RV)), which is exactly the assumption our DP replaces. The empirical anchor for near-optimality of challenge behavior remains Abramitzky, Einav, Kolkowitz & Mill (2012, IER) in tennis.

### 2.1 Zhan, J., Gerstner, L., & Polimeni, J. (2020). "Measuring the Impact of Robotic Umpires." MIT SSAC 2020 research paper (Univ. of Rochester). PDF: https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/5f1af76ed86d6771ad48324b/5f6a65851d1ac98081a707f0_Zhan_Measuring-the-impact-of-robotic-umpires.pdf ; landing: https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/measuring-the-impact-of-robotic-umpires  [READ IN FULL]
- Data: Kaggle MLB pitch data 2015–2018, N = 2,867,154 pitches, 40 features; excludes swings, intentional balls, fouls ("pitches whose outcome is not determined by the umpire").
- Zone: **fixed** — "the width of home plate, 17 inches, plus a half inch on each side to account for the width of the baseball, and ... a constant height of the pitch" (no batter-height adjustment; they concede in Future Work that "a better approach could be to use a dynamic strike zone that adapts to each hitter's height").
- Method: RE24 = RE_end − RE_start + runs; RE12 = average RE24 by count (Table 3: 0-2 −0.114, 1-2 −0.102, 2-2 −0.080, 0-1 +0.030, 1-1 +0.045, 3-2 +0.050, 0-0 +0.053, 2-1 +0.057, 1-0 +0.059, 2-0 +0.078, 3-1 +0.193, 3-0 +0.248).
- Findings: umpires ~90% correct on called pitches; **each missed call ≈ 0.065 runs** ("fifteen missed calls in one game = 1 additional expected run"); miscalled balls +0.12 runs, miscalled strikes −0.10; ~3× more strikes miscalled as balls than vice versa; accuracy by pitch type 87.4% (knuckleball) to 93.0% (splitter); errors concentrate in "the corners of the strike zone, most specifically the top two corners"; pitcher-level (Kershaw 2018: 5.4% of pitches miscalled as strikes vs 2.3% league).
- Relation to us: the "0.065 runs per missed call" benchmark; the count-dependence of a call's value; the fixed-zone flaw we fix (height-scaled ABS zone, mid-plate plane). Positioning line: they price a *universal* robot zone; we price a *scarce, renewable* right to invoke it.
- Undone: no batter-height zone, no win-probability, no strategic decision, no umpire response.

### 2.5 Baseball Savant — ABS metrics documentation, dashboard, leaderboard. https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/abs-metrics-documentation ; https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/abs ; leaderboard CSV: https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/leaderboard/abs-challenges?challengeType=catching-team&level=mlb&gameType=regular&year=2026&sort=n_challenges&sortDir=desc&page=0&pageSize=50&dataMode=for&csv=true  [READ IN FULL]
- **Challenge Opportunity**: "Any pitch where a player is allowed, by the rulebook, to make a challenge": "a called pitch (i.e., ball or strike, no swings)", "at least one challenge is remaining", "the call went against you (a ball for pitcher/catcher, or a strike for batter)"; excludes position-player pitching and technical ABS failures; ≈50% of 2026 pitches.
- **Reasonable Pitch** (≈5% of pitches): an opportunity where (1) "the umpire's call was mistaken (i.e., would be overturned by ABS)", or (2) "was a correct call, yet within 3 inches and had a run value of at least +0.30" (RE288), or (3) "was a correct call, yet had a challenge probability of at least 20%."
- **Confidence Level**: "a scoring system that takes into account the situation (count, outs, and runners on, i.e., the RE288 system)"; "Each pitch gets a run value using the RE288 chart. The breakeven point of a challenge is [0.2 / (0.2 + run value of situation)]." → an implicit constant token cost of 0.2 runs. (Documentation does not justify 0.2 — do not cite it as an estimate.)
- Framing: umpire's initial call sets framing run value; original-vs-final difference is a separate "challenge skill" run value.
- Dashboard (as of Aug 17, 2026): 7,974 attempts, 4,275 overturned (54%); batters 3,666/1,777 (48%); fielders 4,308/2,498 (58%). Zone stated as 17 in, 53.5%/27% of *measured* height, mid-plate. Filters: 2025–2026, **MLB and AAA**, regular/playoff/spring.

### 2.15 Lee, K., Han, K., & Ko, J. (2025). "Analyzing the impact of the automatic ball strike system in professional baseball through a case study on KBO league data." Scientific Reports 15: 44459. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-28142-y (arXiv 2407.15779)  [READ IN FULL]
- KBO 2024 full ABS: width 47.18 cm (43.18 cm plate + 2 cm each side), top 56.35% / bottom 27.64% of height, **two planes** ("one at the middle of the home plate and another at the back of the strike zone ... a strike only when passing both"). Data: 2,515 games 2021–2024 (Naver Sports), 42 umpires; 122,632 ABS pitches (408 games to the 2024 ASB).
- Model: P(strike) = logit⁻¹(−β(d−α)) with p-norm distance (center, aspect λ, consistency β, rectilinearity r). ABS: much higher β and r, narrower width, slightly higher. Gray-zone (0.25-ft bands) strike rates 2023→2024: high-in-zone 57.28%→79.77%; low-in-zone 54.43%→68.83%; low-out 10.49%→0.58%; high curveballs 46.46%→84.56%; inside forkballs vs RHB 53.63%→97.72%. ## 4. Claims we must NOT overclaim / traps
- **Statcast coordinate break in 2026**: plate_x/plate_z moved from front-of-plate to mid-plate and sz_top/sz_bot became ABS-defined. Any 2025-vs-2026 umpire-accuracy or zone comparison must re-derive location at a common plane (or use FG/Clemens' height-normalized approach) — otherwise "umpires got more accurate" is an artifact.
- **Two shocks, not one**: 2025 already tightened umpire grading (2 in → ±0.75 in) and shadow-zone CS% fell to record lows before ABS; 2026 adds challenge accountability. Attribute carefully; Triple-A 2023–24 rotation and 2025 challenge-only seasons help separate them.
- **Role success rates are selection-laden**: catchers challenge closer/different pitches, at different counts and leverage (defense challenge rate ≈2.3% vs batters ≈4.5% of opportunities); do not read 58% vs 48% as raw perceptual skill.
- **The 9th-inning dump is not per se irrational**: use-it-or-lose-it lowers the optimal threshold late; the test is revealed threshold vs DP threshold, not raw success rate.
- **Savant's 0.2 constant** is a modeling convention, not MLB's estimate of token value; do not cite it as "the" value of a challenge.
- **Zone history**: 2023 Triple-A top was 51% (not 53.5%); 2022 width 19 in; KBO uses buffer + two planes; NCAA 58/23%. Don't say "the same zone since 2019."
- **Source discrepancies**: spring-2025 role rates (MLB 56/50/41 vs AP 53.7/45), seconds per challenge (13.8 vs 17), Triple-A 2024 success (50.6 vs 49.5). Cite MLB's official numbers and footnote AP.
- Zhan et al.'s 0.065 runs is per *fixed-zone* miss on 2015–18 data; not comparable to RE288 values on the ABS zone.
- The MLB.com "player survey" is a beat-writer poll; the 54%/8% preference figure comes via the FAccT paper's citation of an MLB survey — verify the primary before quoting.
- Height: zone uses *measured/certified* height (spring 2026), not listed height; FanGraphs notes hundreds of minor leaguers "shrunk" on re-measurement.

## 5. Open questions we can answer (gaps confirmed)
1. State-dependent value of a token (WP DP) vs Savant's constant 0.2 — how far off, and does the DP reproduce the observed rise in challenge rate from 3.76 to 4.60/game as teams learn?
2. Revealed thresholds by role/inning/tokens-in-hand vs optimal; how much of the 9th-inning success collapse (59%→35%) is optimal dumping vs excess?
3. Selection-corrected perception by role (C/B/P) — using distance-from-edge and count controls; whether pitchers' 37% is worse perception or worse pitch selection.
4. Umpire response to accountability: called zone at edges on 2026 MLB vs 2025 (net of buffer change and coordinate change), and Triple-A challenge vs full-ABS days 2023–24 (same umpires, same weeks).
5. Framing × challenge equilibrium (Andrews' inverse pattern is suggestive, unmodeled).
6. Rule counterfactuals: 1/2/3 tokens (NCAA 3; Triple-A 3→2), no-retention, coach-initiated, KBO-style buffer/two-plane zone, 58/23 vs 53.5/27.
7. Value of extra-inning grants; how many team-games end with unused tokens (9% use zero; 33% exactly two).
