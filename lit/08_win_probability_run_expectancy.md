# 08 — Win probability, run expectancy, count-level values, leverage: the measurement stack for pricing a call

*Reader notes for SSAC27 ABS-challenge paper. Compiled 2026-08-17. Web search budget was exhausted before this domain; everything below was fetched directly by URL and read (FanGraphs, THT, Savant, Crossref metadata, arXiv, GitHub). Tango's tangotiger.com/.net pages were rate-limited/robots-blocked — flagged for manual reading.*

## 1. Domain summary

Everything we do to "price" a ball/strike flip sits on a 65-year stack: Lindsey (1959, 1961, 1963) built the first base-out run-expectancy (RE) and score-progress tables from hand-scored games; the OR literature (D'Esopo–Lefkowitz, Cover–Keilers, Bukiet–Harold–Palacios 1997) formalized the half-inning as a Markov chain; the Mills brothers (1970) and later Studeman/Tango/Drinen turned win-expectancy (WE) tables into WPA and Leverage Index (LI); Tango–Lichtman–Dolphin's *The Book* (2007) supplied the canonical RE24 matrix, run values by count and the LI recipe; openWAR (2015) and *Analyzing Baseball Data with R* made RE24 estimation reproducible from public play-by-play; and Statcast operationalized a per-pitch RE288 (24 base-out × 12 count states) as `delta_run_exp` / "Run Value." The 2026 ABS analyses at FanGraphs (Clemens, Oler, Martell, Baumann) and Oyster Analytics all price a challenge as *P(overturn) × ΔRE288* with, at most, an ad-hoc opportunity cost — nobody has published a per-pitch WP288 table, a pitch-level LI, or a finite-horizon renewable-token DP that prices the *option* of a retained challenge in win probability. That is exactly our gap. Standard conversions: ~9.4–10.3 runs per win depending on year (FanGraphs Guts), and roughly $10–12M per free-agent WAR in 2024–26 (Clemens 2026), with steep tiering by player quality.

## 2. Per-work entries

### 2.1 Lindsey, G. R. (1959, 1961, 1963) — origins
- **Citations (Crossref-verified):** "Statistical Data Useful for the Operation of a Baseball Team," *Operations Research* 7(2):197–207, 1959, doi:10.1287/opre.7.2.197. "The Progress of the Score During a Baseball Game," *JASA* 56(295):703–728, 1961, doi:10.1080/01621459.1961.10480656 (also 10.2307/2282091). "An Investigation of Strategies in Baseball," *Operations Research* 11(4):477–501, 1963, doi:10.1287/opre.11.4.477.
- **Data/method:** hand-scored MLB games (Lindsey and his father), tabulating runs scored from each of the 24 base-out states and the distribution of score by inning; used to evaluate sacrifice, intentional walk, steal, and to define "an index of batting effectiveness based on the contribution to run production in average situations" (1963 abstract — the ancestor of linear weights/RE24). 1959 abstract also reports the platoon effect (+0.032 for opposite-hand batters).
- **Relation to us:** RE24 and the idea of pricing an event as ΔRE trace to Lindsey; the 1961 paper is the first in-game score-progress (proto-WP) model. Cite once in the measurement section.

### 2.2 Bukiet, Harold & Palacios (1997); Albert (2015) — Markov / run-distribution lineage
- Bukiet, B., Harold, E. R., Palacios, J. L. "A Markov Chain Approach to Baseball," *Operations Research* 45(1):14–23, 1997, doi:10.1287/opre.45.1.14 — half-inning as a Markov chain over 24 states + absorbing 3-out state; run distributions, batting order, season wins.
- Albert, J. "Beyond runs expectancy," *Journal of Sports Analytics* 1(1):3–18, 2015, doi:10.3233/JSA-140001 — extends Lindsey to the *distribution* of runs by state via multinomial/proportional-odds multilevel models (2013 season; 1998–2013 trends); e.g., ~57% shrinkage of team estimates toward league mean; RISP adds 2.395 to log-odds of scoring.
- **Use:** justify a Markov (compositional) construction of RE288/WP288 rather than raw cell means, and justify shrinkage for sparse (inning × score × base-out × count) cells.

### 2.3 Mills & Mills (1970); Studeman (2004) — WP/WPA history and table construction
- Mills, E. & Mills, H. *Player Win Averages* (A.S. Barnes, 1970) — first WPA.
- Studeman, D. "The One About Win Probability," *The Hardball Times*, Dec 27, 2004 (https://tht.fangraphs.com/the-one-about-win-probability/). Lineage: Mills brothers → Lindsey → Bennett's "Player Game Percentage" → Drinen's WPA → Tango's WE. Two ways to build WE tables: (i) empirical frequencies (Christopher Shea's Win Expectancy Finder, Retrosheet 1979–90) and (ii) "Markov Chains" seeded with base-out run distributions. WPA credit: +.16/−.16 symmetric; winners sum to +.5. Quote: "The talent is the ability to hit the home run; when it happens in a game is something that is pretty random."
- Also: Winston, *Mathletics* (Princeton UP; ch. "Win Probability Added") as a textbook cite; Albert & Bennett, *Curve Ball* (2001/2003) for Lindsey/Markov exposition.

### 2.4 Tango (2006) "Crucial Situations" I–III + FanGraphs Library LI/WPA/WE — how LI is computed
- Tango, T., *THT* 2006 (https://tht.fangraphs.com/crucial-situations/, .../crucial-situations-part-2/). LI = "the swing in the possible change in win probability." Recipe: for the current (inning, half, score, base, outs) enumerate PA outcomes, take |ΔWE| for each, weight by outcome frequency ("the spread is .069 × .67 + .140 × .33 = .092"), divide by the league-average swing, **0.0346 wins**, so "The average swing value, by definition, will equal to 1.00." Part 2 formula: LI = Σ freq(i)·|win impact here(i)| / Σ freq(i)·|win impact random(i)|. Example values: 9th, tie, bases empty LI 2.4; bottom 9th, man on 3rd, 1 out, tie LI 4.5.
- FanGraphs Library LI (https://library.fangraphs.com/misc/li/): "Leverage Index is essentially a measure of how critical a particular situation is. To calculate it, you are measuring the swing of the possible change in win expectancy." Scale avg 1.0; high >2.0; low <0.85; range ~0–10.
- FanGraphs WPA (https://library.fangraphs.com/misc/wpa/): WPA "captures the change in Win Expectancy from one plate appearance to the next"; "It doesn't tell you how well a player performed, it tells you how important their performance was."
- FanGraphs WE (https://library.fangraphs.com/misc/we/): WE = "the percent chance a particular team will win based on the score, inning, outs, runners on base, and the run environment," built from "all similar situations in the last ten years or so"; "Our WE numbers start at 50% for each club"; "standard win probability only knows the inning, score, and base-out state." WE data licensed from TangoTiger.com.
- **Relation:** our pitch-level LI is the same recipe applied at a count state with a WP288 table. Nobody publishes that; FanGraphs LI/WPA are PA-level.

### 2.5 Tango, Lichtman & Dolphin (2007) *The Book* + tangotiger.net RE24 tables

Use era-matched tables (2016+ or 2023+ for the pitch-clock/ABS era).

### 2.6 Walsh, J. (2008) — the count-value table (numbers)
- "Searching for the game's best pitch," *THT*, Feb 26, 2008 (https://tht.fangraphs.com/searching-for-the-games-best-pitch/). Batting runs relative to 0-0, 2007 linear weights: 1-0 +.038; 2-0 +.104; 3-0 +.220; 0-1 −.044; 1-1 −.015; 2-1 +.037; 3-1 +.142; 0-2 −.106; 1-2 −.082; 2-2 −.039; 3-2 +.059. Quote: "the highest leverage occurs on the 3-2 count, where a ball results in a walk and a strike results in a strikeout." (Matches the "3-0 ≈ +0.2, 0-2 ≈ −0.1" folklore from *The Book*, 1999–2002.) Implied context-neutral flip values (ball vs strike): 0-0 ≈ .08, 2-1 ≈ .20, 2-2 ≈ .3+, 3-2 ≈ .6 (BB ≈ +.32 vs K ≈ −.28).

### 2.7 Fast (2011) / Turkenkopf — 0.13 runs per called strike
- Fast, M. "Spinning Yarn: Removing the Mask" (I Was Framed), *Baseball Prospectus*, 2011 (https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/15093/...). Framing runs = net extra strikes × **"0.13 runs on average"** (source: Dan Turkenkopf's count-weighted value of a strike vs ball). Best catcher +73 runs total (35 per 120 g); Doumit −66 (−26 per 120 g). This 0.13 is the standard *context-neutral, called-pitch-weighted* price of a flip and the number Zhan et al.'s 0.065 must be reconciled against (theirs is per *missed call*, likely half-swing/pre-pitch delta — verify before citing side by side).

### 2.8 Baumer, Jensen & Matthews (2015) openWAR — reproducible RE24
- "openWAR: An open source system for evaluating overall player performance in major league baseball," *JQAS* 11(2):69–84, 2015, doi:10.1515/jqas-2014-0098; arXiv:1312.7158; R package github.com/beanumber/openWAR. RE24 from MLBAM GameDay: "The value of ρ(o,b) is estimated as the empirical average of the number of runs scored (until the end of the half-inning) whenever a game was in state (o,b)"; PA value δ = Δρ + r ("often referred to … as RE24"); runs per win "= 2r/(81p)" ≈ 10; interval estimates by resampling — a template for bootstrapping our RE288/WP288.
- Related reproducibility: Marchi, Albert & Baumer, *Analyzing Baseball Data with R* (2nd ed., CRC 2018; code github.com/beanumber/baseball_R): ch. 4 runs↔wins, ch. 5 RE24 from Retrosheet, ch. 6 "Balls and Strikes Effects" (run values by count from pitch sequences), ch. 7 catcher framing.

### 2.9 Singer (2020) — RE288 pricing of missed calls (Umpire Scorecards' engine)
- Singer, E. "The Effect of Umpires on Baseball: Umpire Runs Created (URC)," FanGraphs Community, Apr 30, 2020 (https://community.fangraphs.com/the-effect-of-umpires-on-baseball-umpire-runs-created-urc/). Data: Baseball Savant pitch-by-pitch, 2019. Definitions: RE288 = "Run expectancy for all 288 base-out-count combinations (0, 1, or 2 outs, 8 different base situations, and 12 possible counts)"; RED = |RE(strike) − RE(ball)|; uRC = "The summation of all incorrect calls and their respective REDs"; NuRC = home − away. Numbers: bases loaded/0 out/3-0 → 2.81 runs; empty/2 out/0-2 → 0.06; "the Leverage of a 3-2 pitch with the bases loaded and two outs is 1.74 runs"; "The mean RED of all pitches was 0.32 runs, while the median was 0.24 runs"; "33,277 incorrect calls" in 2019, "13.8 per game, or just over 1.5 per inning"; "about 733 total runs lost in the season"; 58 games would flip. Limitations noted: point-location zone (no ball radius stated), no error margin.
- **Relation:** the exact RED construct we need per pitch; also the popular "run impact/favor" numbers on umpscorecards.com. Note the internal tension (mean RED 0.32 vs 733 total runs/33k calls ≈ 0.022) — do not quote either without re-deriving.

### 2.10 Statcast / MLB data definitions (Savant, StatsAPI)
- Savant CSV docs (https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/csv-docs): `delta_run_exp` = "The change in Run Expectancy before the Pitch and after the Pitch"; `delta_home_win_exp` = "The change in Win Expectancy before the Plate Appearance and after the Plate Appearance"; `home_win_exp`, `bat_win_exp`; `sz_top/sz_bot` "ABS-defined from 2026 onward"; `plate_x/plate_z` "middle-of-plate from 2026 onward" (comparability trap for 2015–25 vs 2026). `delta_pitcher_run_exp` appears in CSVs but is undocumented.
- Savant "Run Value" definition (pitch-arsenal page): "the run impact of an event based on the runners on base, outs, ball and strike count," linking to Tango's tangotiger.com/index.php/site/article/run-values-by-pitch-count. So Statcast RV on a taken pitch is exactly RE288(new count) − RE288(old count) with base-out context; useful as an external check on our table.
- StatsAPI `GET /api/v1/game/{gamePk}/winProbability`: per plate appearance `homeTeamWinProbability`, `awayTeamWinProbability`, `homeTeamWinProbabilityAdded`, `leverageIndex`, `dramaIndex`, `atBatIndex` (verified on gamePk 823990). Savant `/gf?game_pk=` feed: per-pitch records with `is_abs_challenge`; WPA only per PA. **No public per-pitch WP exists** → WP288 is a genuine contribution.
- Savant ABS Challenges leaderboard (https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/leaderboard/abs-challenges, CSV via `&csv=true`): challenge rate = "rate of challenges out of challengeable pitches"; "Reasonable challenge opportunities" = call incorrect, or within 3 in. of edge with 0.3+ run gain, or ≥20% expected-challenge rate; an "expected challenges" model uses location, challenges remaining, runners, count. Columns include n_challenges, n_overturns, exp_chal, exp_rate_overturns, net_chal_gained/lost, n_strikeouts_flip, n_walks_flip (and *_against). No run-value column.

### 2.11 Clemens, B. (2026) "An Early, Nerdy Look At The Challenge System," FanGraphs, Apr 1, 2026
- https://blogs.fangraphs.com/an-early-nerdy-look-at-the-challenge-system/. ### 2.14 Oyster Analytics (Lane & Riley, 2026) — break-even thresholds with opportunity cost
- https://oysteranalytics.com/ (Challenge Dashboard, Break-Evens, Glossary); paywalled guide: "The Oyster Guide to ABS Challenge," *Down on the Farm* Substack, Feb 25, 2026; Effectively Wild ep. 2444 (Feb 25, 2026). Findings claimed: teams under-challenge; hitters gain more on high pitches.
- **Relation:** closest practitioner analog to our DP (runs-based, opaque, non-peer-reviewed, no published tables). We must cite it and differentiate: WP objective, explicit finite-horizon Bellman recursion, revealed-vs-optimal thresholds, counterfactual rule design.

## 4. Claims we must NOT overclaim / traps
- "The value of a call" has at least four different published magnitudes: 0.13 runs (Turkenkopf/Fast; context-neutral flip, called-pitch weighted), 0.065 runs per missed call (Zhan et al. 2020 — likely a pre-pitch-to-post-call delta, i.e., roughly half a flip), Singer's mean RED 0.32/median 0.24 (RE288 with base-out, "all pitches"), and Oler's 2026 distribution (two-thirds of challengeable pitches < 0.13 runs, <10% > 0.33). Always state population (all / called / missed / challenged), reference (vs correct call = full flip; vs pre-pitch = half), and whether base-out is included.
- RE288 ignores inning and score; runs → wins only via LI ≈ 1. Clemens's "a run is a run" is defensible for context-neutral player evaluation, not for a scarce token that expires at game end. Our claim should be that WP and RE recommendations diverge specifically late/close and by tokens remaining — show it, don't assert it.
- WPA/LI are PA-level; there is no public pitch-level LI or WP. Say "we construct" rather than "we use FanGraphs'."
- WE tables assume average teams starting at 50%; FanGraphs' own text concedes it "doesn't know anything about who is playing." Our DP inherits that.
- Estimation noise: RE288 has 288 cells (WP288 has thousands); use era-matched data (RE24 fell from .547 to .481 for empty/0-out between 1993–2009 and 2010–15) and bootstrap (Brill et al.).
- Statcast 2026 redefinitions: `plate_x/plate_z` now "middle-of-plate," `sz_top/sz_bot` "ABS-defined" — geometry is not comparable to 2015–25 rows without care; `delta_home_win_exp` is per PA even though it sits on pitch rows.
- Success rate ≠ value (Oler, Baumann): high-leverage challenges succeed less often but earn more; "Every 11.6 challenges … correlates to an additional run" is a cross-sectional correlation, not a causal return to aggressiveness.
- Runs per win is 9.4–10.3 by year, not a flat 10; $/WAR is a free-agent market price, tiered and non-linear — a "challenge program is worth $X" statement needs both caveats.
- Framing comparison: challenge effects (≈1 win best-to-worst) are an order of magnitude smaller than 2008 framing gaps (97.7 runs) — do not oversell the economic stakes.
- Singer's numbers contain an internal inconsistency (mean RED vs total runs); re-derive rather than quote.

## 5. Open questions we can answer
- Publish the first per-pitch WP288 table (inning × half × outs × bases × score × count) with bootstrap CIs, and a pitch-level LI; quantify how often WP- and RE-optimal challenge decisions disagree, and where (score, inning, tokens).
- Replace the flat ~0.1-run option value (Clemens) with the DP's state-dependent continuation value; report break-even P(overturn) surfaces by (inning, tokens, count, base-out) — a public version of Oyster's tool.
- Validate our RE288 against Statcast `delta_run_exp` on called pitches (should match cell-for-cell) and against Umpire Scorecards' RED.
- Convert the 2026 realized challenge runs (Baumann: 18–34 per team) into WP-weighted wins and dollars using year-specific R/W and $/WAR; test whether WPA-weighting changes team rankings.
- Show that FanGraphs' "runs not wins" recommendation is approximately right in the middle innings and wrong in the 8th–9th (the dump), using our DP.
