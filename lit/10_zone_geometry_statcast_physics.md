# 10 — Strike-zone geometry & Statcast physics for reconstructing the ABS zone from public data

Reader: domain 10. Date: 2026-08-17. Web-search budget was exhausted before this domain started, so everything below comes from direct fetches of known URLs, FanGraphs/BA site search, MLB StatsAPI and Baseball Savant JSON endpoints, plus local numerical checks. Items marked [VERIFIED HERE] are computations I ran on live feed data; re-run them in the pipeline.

## 1. Domain summary

The ABS zone is a legislated object, not the rulebook prism: a two-dimensional 17-in-wide rectangle in the vertical plane through the *middle* of home plate (8.5 in behind the front edge), bottom/top at 27%/53.5% of the batter's *certified measured* height, strike "if any part of the ball touches any part of the strike zone" (MLB press release, Sept 23 2025; Shenk, MLB.com, Feb 5 2026) — so the ball's centre may lie up to one ball radius (1.43–1.47 in) outside the rectangle (rounded corners), with no tolerance/buffer (Umpire Scorecards). Public Statcast data reproduce this ruling almost exactly: pitch locations are deterministic functions of the published nine-parameter (9P) constant-acceleration fit (x0,y0≈50 ft,z0,vx0,vy0,vz0,ax,ay,az) — I reproduced Savant plate_x/plate_z and StatsAPI pX/pZ to 1e-4 ft from the 9P for MLB 2025, MLB 2026, AAA 2023 and AAA 2024 pitches — so any pitch can be moved to any plane with the quadratic-time formula used by pitchRx/sabRmetrics. Three regime facts: (i) MLB Savant plate_x/plate_z were front-of-plate (y = 17/12 ft) through 2025 and mid-plate (y = 8.5/12 ft) from 2026 (Savant CSV docs), whereas the StatsAPI live feed's pX/pZ are *still front-of-plate in 2026*; Triple-A Savant data have been mid-plate since 2023; (ii) sz_top/sz_bot are operator-set through MLB 2025 (noisy) but equal the ABS zone (0.535·H, 0.27·H) for AAA 2023+ and MLB 2026, and StatsAPI `people` now publishes per-player `strikeZoneTop/Bottom`, so certified heights are recoverable to ≈0.1 in (H = 12·sz_top/0.535); (iii) moving from front to mid-plane shifts a pitch ≈ −0.6 to −1.3 in vertically and 0–0.7 in horizontally — several times Hawk-Eye's stated accuracy (0.25 in average, MLB Technology Blog 2020; "one-sixth of an inch", MLB.com 2026) — so plane choice, not tracking noise, is the first-order geometry decision. Prior work normalized height with the operator zone (Walsh 2007; Green & Daniels 2014; Deshpande & Wyner 2017; Kim & King 2014); none used a legislated height fraction, a mid-plate plane, or the any-part rule.

### 2.5 Andrews, D. (Dec 5, 2024). "Maybe There's No Such Thing as a Perfectly Fair Strike Zone." FanGraphs. https://blogs.fangraphs.com/maybe-theres-no-such-thing-as-a-perfectly-fair-strike-zone/ [READ IN FULL]
- Zone height = 26.5% of H (6-ft batter: 19.4–38.5 in); Judge's zone "3.45 inches taller than Jose Altuve's"; rulebook diagram's zone "represents a whopping 41% of his crouched height" (would need a 6-9 batter under ABS). Notes "hundreds of minor leaguers suddenly shrank last fall."
- TRAP: his area formula "(22.9 × (26.5% of Height + 5.9)) − 7.4" expands the plate by a full ball *diameter* per side; the any-part rule expands by one *radius* (Walsh 2007: "just a hair under 20 inches (1.66 feet)"). Correct ball-centre area = (17+2r)(0.265H+2r) − (4−π)r² ≈ 472 sq in for Judge (79 in), 404 for Altuve (66 in), 436 for a 6-footer (r = 1.45), not 606.5/527.7; ≈5.3 sq in per inch of stature. Recompute; do not cite his numbers.

### 2.7 [VERIFIED HERE] Savant game-feed JSON (https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/gf?game_pk=…) and StatsAPI (https://statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1.1/game/{pk}/feed/live ; /api/v1/people?personIds=…)
Method: first pitch of four games; propagated the feed's 9P with t(y) = [−vy0 − √(vy0² − 2·ay·(y0 − y))]/ay, x(y)=x0+vx0·t+½ax·t², z(y)=z0+vz0·t+½az·t², and compared with the reported location.
- MLB 2025 (gamePk 777505, Jun 15 2025): Savant px/pz 0.3957/2.0656 = 9P at y=17/12 exactly [mid-plate would be 0.3973/1.9874]; StatsAPI pX/pZ 0.39556/2.06571 (same plane, 1e-4 ft apart); feed strikeZoneWidth 17.0, strikeZoneDepth **17.0**; sz_top/sz_bot 3.1018/1.5561 ft (operator values; implied H 69.6 vs 69.2 in — not one fixed fraction).
- MLB 2026 (gamePk 823561, Apr 15 2026; Gil→Neto): Savant px/pz 0.8707/2.1616 = 9P at y=**8.5/12** exactly; StatsAPI pX/pZ 0.8276/2.2299 = 9P at y=**17/12** exactly → the two public sources sit on different planes in 2026. Feed strikeZoneDepth **8.5**. sz_top/sz_bot 3.17/1.60 ft (2 decimals) → H = 71.10/71.11 in; ratio 1.981 = 0.535/0.27.
- AAA 2024 (gamePk 752322): Savant px/pz −0.4016/3.8662 = 9P at mid-plate; sz 3.121/1.575 → H = 70.00 in from both (3-decimal, exact). AAA 2023 (gamePk 723374): mid-plate; sz 3.018/1.598, ratio 1.8886 = 0.51/0.27 → H = 71.0 in. Savant pitch records carry `is_abs_challenge`; `game_status.hasAbs` was false for both AAA games (unreliable — code full-ABS vs challenge days from the published calendar, not this flag).
- StatsAPI `people` returns `strikeZoneTop/strikeZoneBottom`: Neto 3.17/1.6 (H 71.1; listed 5'11"), Trout 3.244/1.637 (72.8; listed 6'1", formerly 6'2"), Caballero 3.083/1.556 (69.2; 5'9"), Judge 3.523/1.778 (79.0; 6'7") — listed heights now round the certified height. gameData.absChallenges {usedSuccessful, usedFailed, remaining}; playEvents carry details.hasReview.
- Front→mid shift on these pitches: Δx = +0.52, +0.02, +0.65, +0.15 in; Δz = −0.82, −0.94, −0.59, −0.85 in. Transit of the 8.5 in at ≈120 ft/s is ≈6 ms; drag/Magnus variation over 6 ms is negligible (< 0.01 in).

### 2.8 Kagan, D. (2009). "The Anatomy of a Pitch: Doing Physics with PITCHf/x Data." *The Physics Teacher* 47(7): 412–416. (PDF via https://baseball.physics.illinois.edu/KaganPitchfx.pdf) [READ IN FULL]
- "The origin is at the back point of home plate on the ground. The x-axis points to the catcher's right ... The y-axis points directly toward the pitcher so the y-component of the pitched ball's velocity is always negative. The z-axis is oriented upward." Worked 9P example (y0 = 50.0 ft). Sportvision claimed to "measure the position of the baseball to better than ½ inch." Ball weight 0.320 lb.
- Use: the peer-reviewed citation for coordinate conventions (front edge at y = 17/12 ft, midpoint at 8.5/12 ft follow from Rule 2.02's 17-in plate depth).

### 2.9 Nathan, A. M. (2008). "A Statistical Study of PITCHf/x Pitched Baseball Trajectories" (https://baseball.physics.illinois.edu/MCAnalysis.pdf); Nathan, "Determining Pitch Movement from PITCHf/x Data" (Movement.pdf); Pendleton, R. (Sportvision, Nov 26 2011) "Pitchf/x Nine-Parameter Trajectory Model Fitting" (PitchFX_9P_Model-4.pdf); index https://baseball.physics.illinois.edu/pitchtracker.html [READ IN FULL]
- Monte Carlo with 1-in rms per-frame noise: plate-crossing "rms values are small, ≈0.55 inches"; movement "≈2.3 inches"; speed 0.4 mph. Model: "x_M(t) = [Xo,Yo,Zo] + (t−to)[Vx,Vy,Vz] + ½(t−to)²[Ax,Ay,Az]"; "each trajectory is parametrized by nine numbers ... constant acceleration"; "origin x=y=z=0 is at the corner of home plate, the y axis points toward the pitcher, the z axis vertically up, and the x axis to the catcher's right." Nathan's trajectory calculators (TrajectoryCalculator.xlsx; trajectory-calculator-new3D.html) are drag/Magnus ODE tools for full flights, not needed for the 8.5-in step.
- Use: the 9P is a *fit* — plate location is a derived quantity (2.7 confirms empirically), so plane changes are exact re-evaluations of one polynomial, not new measurements.

### 2.10 Jedlovec, B. (Jul 20, 2020). "Introducing Statcast 2020: Hawk-Eye and Google Cloud." MLB Technology Blog. https://technology.mlblogs.com/introducing-statcast-2020-hawk-eye-and-google-cloud-a5f5c20321b8 [READ IN FULL]
- "12 cameras ... Five cameras operating at 100 frames per second are primarily dedicated to pitch tracking"; "The measured location of the center of the baseball at the front of home plate has been found in repeated testing to be accurate within 0.25 inches on average." Spin measured directly.
- Use: the accuracy figure for a measurement-error band (|d| ≲ 0.25–0.5 in = coin flip); Hawk-Eye tennis benchmark 3.6 mm (AEKM 2012, lit/01) for context. Sievert (2.12) reminds that whole-trajectory *reconstruction* error is larger ("within a couple of inches").

### 2.11 Umpire Scorecards, explainers "/page/info/explainers/accuracy" and "/page/info/explainers/abs" (umpscorecards.com) [READ IN FULL]
- 2026 method: "each player's officially measured strike zone dimensions, apply no tolerance or margin of error", "project each pitch's location to the middle of the plate using pitch trajectory data"; "agreed with the ABS decision more than 99.5 percent of the time" on "roughly 1,600 pitches challenged during spring training in 2025 and 2026." ABS page: "exactly 17 inches wide, the top of the zone is exactly 53.5% of a player's height, and the bottom is 27%"; "measured as the ball crosses the center of the plate, exactly 8.5 inches from the plate's front and back"; "there appears to be no margin of error." Pre-2026: Monte Carlo, "500 potential true locations", 90% rule.
- Use: independent proof that public 9P + measured height + mid-plane + no buffer reproduces ABS rulings; our validation on 2026 overturns should reach ≥99% agreement (residual disagreements = |d| within ~0.1 in).

### 2.12 Sievert, C. (2014). "Taming PITCHf/x Data with XML2R and pitchRx." *The R Journal* 6(1): 5–19; code https://github.com/cpsievert/pitchRx (R/getSnapshots.R) [READ IN FULL]
- "a quadratic regression model with nine parameters (defined by the equations of motion for constant linear acceleration) is fit to each pitch"; "often provides a reasonable estimate (within a couple of inches) of the actual locations." Code: `times <- (-vy0 - sqrt(vy0^2 - 2*ay*(y0 - 1.417)))/ay` (1.417 ft = front of plate), then x0+vx0·t+0.5·ax·t², etc.
- Powers, S., sabRmetrics (GPL-3; https://github.com/saberpowers/sabRmetrics): `get_quadratic_coef()`, `get_trackman_metrics()` — "plate_y = 17 / 12, # back of home plate is zero; front is 17 inches"; "the vast majority of well-known pitch tracking metrics (e.g. horizontal/vertical break, plate location, approach angle) are derived from this estimated quadratic trajectory." It anchors the polynomial at release_pos with velocities back-tracked from y=50; for plate work anchor at plate_x/plate_z (plane known by source/season) instead, since release_pos need not lie on the 9P. baseballr `statcast_search()` and pybaseball `statcast()` download only.

### 2.15 Deshpande, S. K. & Wyner, A. (2017). "A hierarchical Bayesian model of pitch framing." *JQAS* 13(3): 95–112; arXiv:1704.00823 [READ arXiv IN FULL]
- They "approximate a crude 'average rule book strike zone' by averaging the vertical strike zone boundaries recorded by the PITCHf/x system across all players and all pitches" and keep N = 308,388 taken pitches "within one foot" of it; location enters as a GAM smooth of historical called-strike log-odds by handedness; ball radius ≈1.45 in noted for borderline definitions. No height normalization, no measurement-error treatment.
- Kim & King (2014, *Mgmt Sci* 60(11)) operationalize the rulebook zone as |px| ≤ 0.83 ft and sz_bot ≤ pz ≤ sz_top (lit/04) — the standard "plate + radius, operator top/bottom" convention we depart from.

### 2.16 Freiman, N. (Aug 16, 2018). "How Much Does Height Affect a Hitter's Zone?" FanGraphs. https://blogs.fangraphs.com/how-much-does-height-affect-a-hitters-zone/ [READ IN FULL]
- Operator sz_top/sz_bot vary pitch to pitch within a hitter (he uses per-hitter medians); tall hitters get more low strikes (height R² = 0.23; top recipients average 75.4 in) but height barely predicts high strikes (R² = 0.05). Use: shows the operator zone is a noisy proxy and that called zones vary with stature (Andrews Dec 2024 concurs).

### 2.17 Official Baseball Rules (2021 ed.; https://img.mlbstatic.com/mlb-images/image/upload/mlb/atcjzj9j7wrgvsm8wnjq.pdf) & MLB glossary "Strike Zone" (https://www.mlb.com/glossary/rules/strike-zone) [READ]
- Rule 3.01 (p. 5): the ball "shall weigh not less than five nor more than 5¼ ounces avoirdupois and measure not less than nine nor more than 9¼ inches in circumference" → diameter 2.865–2.944 in, radius 1.43–1.47 in (use 1.45 in; sensitivity ±0.02 in). Rule 2.02 (p. 3): home base "a 17-inch square with two of the corners removed so that one edge is 17 inches long, two adjacent sides are 8½ inches and the remaining two sides are 12 inches" → depth 17 in, midpoint 8.5 in. Definitions: strike zone = "that area over home plate the upper limit of which is a horizontal line at the midpoint between the top of the shoulders and the top of the uniform pants, and the lower level is a line at the hollow beneath the kneecap ... determined from the batter's stance as the batter is prepared to swing" (glossary paraphrase; history 1950/1963/1969/1988/1996).

## 4. Claims we must NOT overclaim / traps
- **Plane mismatch by source and season.** MLB Savant plate_x/z: front ≤2025, mid ≥2026. StatsAPI feed pX/pZ: front in 2026 too (verified Apr 15 2026). AAA Savant: mid since 2023. `code/abs_zone.py` treats plate_x/plate_z as front-of-plate for all inputs → for MLB 2026 and AAA it would shift pitches a second 8.5 in (≈ −0.8 in vertical bias). Fix: branch on (source, season) or re-derive from the 9P (x0,z0 from the feed, or anchor at plate_x/z with the correct plane).
- **Corner geometry.** Any-part rule = ball centre within r of the rectangle (rounded corners). Expanding the rectangle by r and testing containment (as abs_zone.py does) wrongly adds the four corner squares ((4−π)r² ≈ 1.8 sq in). Compute d = dist(centre, unexpanded rectangle) − r (inside: −inset − r).
- **Heights.** StatsAPI listed heights now round the certified height; the certified height is recoverable from people.strikeZoneTop (÷0.535) and from 2026/AAA sz_top/sz_bot (MLB 2026 rounded to 0.01 ft → H ± ~0.1 in; AAA 3-decimal, exact). Pre-2023 listed heights run ~1–2 in high (Longenhagen) — do not use them for retrospective ABS zones without saying so.
- **Andrews' zone-area formula** doubles the ball expansion; Walsh's ≈19.9-in width is the correct any-part width.
- **Accuracy figures are not interchangeable**: Sportvision "better than ½ inch" (2009); Nathan's 0.55-in rms is a simulation with 1-in per-frame noise; Hawk-Eye "0.25 in on average" (2020) is at the *front* of the plate; "one-sixth of an inch" (2026) is a media paraphrase; Sievert's "couple of inches" is whole-track reconstruction. Report a ±0.25–0.5 in coin-flip band and never claim ABS "truth" beyond Hawk-Eye's precision (Collins & Evans point, lit/01).
- **Zone history**: 2022 AAA 19 in wide; 2023 51%/27%; Sept 5–end 2023 stance-based (knees; hips + two balls); 2024+ 53.5%/27%; NCAA 58/23 (2027); KBO buffer/two planes. Never write "the same zone since 2019."
- **sz_top/sz_bot semantics flip in 2026** — an umpire "accuracy" series that uses sz_top/sz_bot as truth changes definition (operator → legislated) exactly when the challenge system starts; recompute all years with one zone (legislated fractions on measured/derived heights, mid-plane).
- 53.5%/27% is *not* the average operator zone (2025 example: 54.7%/27.5% of listed height; Clemens 2026: the ABS top sits below the historically called top). Don't call the ABS zone "the rulebook zone."

## 5. Open questions we could answer
1. Exact reproduction rate of ABS rulings from public 9P + derived heights, by |d| band, ball radius (1.43/1.45/1.47) and plane — and the empirical error distribution of Hawk-Eye at the edge (from disagreements among challenged pitches).
2. Size of the front-vs-mid plane effect on umpire "accuracy" series 2020–26 (≈1 in vertical) — a methodological note in itself.
3. Distribution of certified heights vs pre-2023 listed heights (StatsAPI people vs Chadwick/Lahman histories) and its effect on zone area (0.265 in of zone height per inch of stature; ~5 sq in per inch).
4. Stance-based (Sept 2023 AAA) vs height-based zone: how many called pitches change classification and for whom (crouchers vs upright) — a rule-design counterfactual with a real precedent.
5. Whether operator sz_top/sz_bot (2015–25) systematically over/under-state 0.535H/0.27H by batter height, handedness, and stance — reusing Freiman/Andrews' setup with certified heights.
6. Value of a small tolerance band (KBO-style buffer) under a challenge regime: share of challenges decided within ±0.25 in.
