## 1. Domain summary

Since 2023 a small but fast-growing peer-reviewed literature has formed around ABS and around the statistical description of the human called zone, and a referee who types "automated ball-strike" into Crossref will find items our corpus did not cite. Three clusters matter. **(a) KBO 2024 as a natural experiment.** Song, Kang & Paulsen (ESMQ 2026) run a player-level difference-in-differences on 2023 vs 2024 KBO seasons (n = 148 batters, 112 pitchers) and find that full ABS *hurt high-status batters* (OBP, IsoD, BB%, SO%, BB/K) relative to low-status batters while high-status pitchers were unaffected — the first peer-reviewed evidence that a machine zone removes a status bias in favor of prominent hitters (Kim & King 2014 in the field). Cho & Lee (SSRN 2025) study the same season as an event study but bundle ABS with the shift ban, which is exactly the confound that hangs over every 2024-KBO performance result. **(b) The human zone, statistically.** Zimmerman, Tang & Huang (AOAS 2019; >3 M PITCHf/x called pitches 2008–16) introduced outline analysis (kernel discriminant outline → elliptic-Fourier / superellipse fit → regression on shape coefficients); Post, Tang & Zimmerman (JSA 2025; 5.08 M called pitches, 36,527 games, 2008–23) update it: accuracy rose from 85.5% to 91.9%, the called zone matched the rulebook in centroid, eccentricity and rectangularity by 2023 but stayed ~10% too large, and — importantly for us — they explicitly forecast that a *challenge* system at ~8% miss rate (≈11.5 misses/game) "is likely to be successful," using the tennis analogy. Shinya & Tomomura (Research Square, June 2026; 450,460 called RHP–RHB four-seamers 2015–24, 88 umpires) fit probit psychometric functions per count and show that a Bayesian observer with count-specific *called-pitch* priors reproduces the count-dependent PSE shifts at three of four edges (R² 0.94 high, 0.69 outer, 0.52 inner, 0.24 low) and that noisier umpires lean more on the prior — reframing Green & Daniels' count bias as rational integration; they also predict which side gains from challenging in which counts. Fesselmeyer (SEJ 2021) shows umpire accuracy falls ~1 pp (5.5% more errors at a 13.3% base) above 95°F — the cleanest environmental miss-rate shifter for our DP's p. **(c) Perception and organisations.** Chen, Chang & Huang (Cerebral Cortex 2023) put 25 batters, 22 pitchers and 22 non-players in an fMRI scanner judging occluded pitch videos: batters 57%, pitchers 55%, non-players 42% correct; d′ 0.82 / 0.63 / 0.37; strikes were called correctly 66% of the time versus balls 36% — a peer-reviewed prior for role-specific sensitivity and for a "call it a strike" criterion; Chen & Huang (Psychophysiology 2024) find expert umpires "more certain but not more accurate" than intermediates and worse on left-handers. Kamino, Wang, Šabanović & Jung (HICSS 2026) and Kamino et al. (HRI 2026) are the sociotechnical companions to the FAccT paper already in notes 06 (ABS as organisational sensemaking; "what is a robot?"). None of these solves a challenge decision problem, estimates challenge thresholds, or separates role precision from role selection — but each supplies a prior, a method, or a rival explanation we must acknowledge.

### 2.1 Song, J., Kang, J. H., & Paulsen, R. J. (2026). "Technology adoption and bias in officiating: automated Ball-Strike System implementation in Korean Baseball." *European Sport Management Quarterly*, published online July 9, 2026, pp. 1–23. doi:10.1080/16184742.2026.2681111. CC BY-NC-ND (bronze OA at tandfonline; no repository copy per Unpaywall). All three authors University of Michigan; Song corresponding. [ABS+ — verbatim abstract via OpenAlex; full text blocked from this proxy]
- **Abstract (verbatim, structured):** "Research Question: Officiating technologies are increasingly gaining attention in sports leagues as a means to reduce the subjectivity inherent in human umpire decision-making. In 2024, Korea Baseball Organization (KBO) officially implemented Automated Ball-Strike System (ABS)… This technological advancement holds potential to eliminate preexisting biases in decisions, particularly those favoring prominent players. Treating this policy change as a natural experiment, we examine whether ABS adoption differentially affects players' performance based on status. Research Methods: Utilizing data from the 2023 and 2024 KBO seasons (n = 148 for batters; n = 112 for pitchers), we estimate a series of difference-in-differences linear regressions to identify the impact of ABS adoption. Results and Findings: We find that ABS negatively affects high-status batters' – OBP, IsoD, BB%, SO%, BB/K – relative to low-status batters. In contrast, high-status pitchers' performance remains unaffected. Implications: Our findings not only inform sports leagues about unintended effects of officiating technology adoption but also offer broader insights for labor markets where subjective evaluation remains prevalent."
- **Design as far as recoverable:** player-season panel, two seasons, treated group = high-status players (definition not in abstract — likely All-Star/award/salary/tenure; verify), control = low-status; outcomes are plate-discipline aggregates (IsoD = OBP − AVG). 51–69 references (Crossref says 69, OpenAlex 52), incl. Chen–Moskowitz–Shue (2016) and KBO ABS consistency work — i.e., the paper sits in the officiating-bias/status literature (Kim & King 2014 "Seeing Stars"; Parsons et al. 2011; Mills 2014).
- **Relation to us:** (i) peer-reviewed prior that the human zone carried a *status premium for hitters* that a machine zone deletes; under a *challenge* system, that premium survives on every unchallenged pitch — a directly testable heterogeneity in our accountability pillar (does star-batter favoritism shrink in 2026 MLB, in AAA challenge vs full-ABS days, or only when a challenge is actually available?). (ii) It is a performance-outcome DiD, not a pitch-level call analysis — the "bias" is inferred, so cite it as *outcome* evidence. (iii) Endogenous adaptation is baked in (batters' 2024 swing decisions changed; Lee, Han & Ko 2025 say batters had "not yet properly adjusted"), which is precisely the objection to naive counterfactuals in our Pillar 6.
- **Undone:** no pitch-level umpire calls, no umpire heterogeneity, no challenge design; and 2024 KBO also banned shifts (see 2.2) — the DiD's ABS effect on BB%/SO% is plausibly clean of the shift ban, but OBP/IsoD are not obviously so.

### 2.4 Chen, Y.-H., Chang, C.-Y., & Huang, S.-K. (2023). "Strike or ball? Batters know it better: an fMRI study of action anticipation in baseball players." *Cerebral Cortex* 33(6): 3221–3238, doi:10.1093/cercor/bhac271 (online July 4, 2022; PMID 35788641). [FULL — OUP article page]
- **Participants:** 25 batters (20.0 ± 1.6 y), 22 pitchers (21.0 ± 2.9 y) from "highly ranked Taiwanese university teams" (training from ~age 10, 23–25 h/wk), 22 non-players; fMRI sample 24/21/19 after motion exclusions.
- **Task:** temporal-occlusion videos of one model pitcher's four-seam fastballs (120–125 km/h), cut "immediately before ball passed home plate"; 4 runs × 48 trials (44 experimental = 22 strikes + 22 balls, per run); predict strike/ball, then rate confidence 1–4.
- **Behavioral numbers:** accuracy 57% (B) / 55% (P) / 42% (NP), F(2,66) = 11.22, P < .001; d′ 0.82 / 0.63 / 0.37, F(2,63) = 6.84, P = .002; batters > non-players in d′ (CI [0.153, 0.747], P = .001) but "no difference between batters and pitchers"; strikes 66% vs balls 36% correct (F(1,66) = 105.49, P < .001) — a large liberal criterion; no-response 11%/11%/28%; experts more confident on correct trials.
- **fMRI:** all groups engage the action-observation network, putamen, cerebellum; left premotor: P > B > NP; only batters show strike-vs-ball differences in left ventral extrastriate cortex; correct > incorrect trials recruit striatum/thalamus/sensorimotor/cerebellum, most widespread in batters.
- **Quotes:** "perceptual experience might enhance action anticipation ability to a greater extent than motor experience, with overlapping but specific neural underpinnings" (Abstract). "Only batters exhibited differences in strike versus ball pitches in their left ventral extrastriate cortex" (Abstract). Authors themselves flag "the low ecological validity of the temporal occlusion paradigm."
- **Relation:** the best peer-reviewed prior for a *role-specific σ* (batter < pitcher < naive) — better than Bahill/Kishita gaze physics because it measures the strike/ball judgment itself — and for a *criterion* that favors "strike." Trap: pitchers were not significantly worse than batters; the stimulus is a video from the umpire/centre-field vantage, not the batter's box; university players, one pitcher, one pitch type. Use as motivation for ordering B ≥ P in precision, not for magnitudes.

### 2.6 Zimmerman, D. L., Tang, J., & Huang, R. (2019). "Outline analyses of the called strike zone in Major League Baseball." *Annals of Applied Statistics* 13(4): 2416–2451, doi:10.1214/19-AOAS1285. [ABS+ — verbatim abstract via OpenAlex; details via Post et al. 2025; projecteuclid blocked]
- **Abstract (verbatim, lightly repaired):** "We extend statistical shape analytic methods known as outline analysis for application to the strike zone… Although the strike zone is rigorously defined by Major League Baseball's official rules, umpires make mistakes in calling pitches strikes (and balls) and may even adhere to [a zone] somewhat different than that prescribed by [the] rule book. Our methods yield inference on geometric attributes (centroid, dimensions, orientation and shape) of this 'called [strike] zone' (CSZ) and effects that years, umpires, player attributes, situation factors and their interactions have on those attributes. The methodology consists of first using kernel discriminant analysis to determine a noisy outline… then fitting existing elliptic Fourier and new generalized superelliptic models to [the] closed curves and finally analyzing the fitted model coefficients using standard methods of regression analysis, factorial analysis of variance and [variance] component estimation. We apply these methods to PITCHf/x data comprising more than three million called pitches from 2008–2016… We find that all geometric attributes of the CSZ, except its size, became significantly more like those of the rule-book strike zone… We also establish that variation in the horizontal center, width and area of an individual umpire's CSZ pitch to pitch is smaller than variation among CSZs from different umpires."
- **Relation:** the peer-reviewed zone-outline method a stats referee will expect beside Clemens' 2026 kernel contours; the "size did not converge" finding is the baseline for our 2026 called-zone shrinkage claim; the umpire-level variance-component result (between-umpire > within-umpire) supports modelling umpire fixed effects on zone edges. Undone: ends 2016; no Statcast/Hawk-Eye truth; no challenge behaviour.

### 2.7 Post, R., Tang, J., & Zimmerman, D. L. (2025). "On the evolution of the accuracy, within-game consistency, and geometry of the called strike zone in Major League Baseball from 2008–2023." *Journal of Sports Analytics* 11 (Oct 1, 2025), doi:10.1177/22150218251389237. Diamond OA (CC BY-NC). [FULL — Sage full text]
- **Data:** 5,083,585 called pitches, 36,527 regular-season games, 2008–2023 (PITCHf/x 2008–16; TrackMan 2017–23); pitchouts and dirt pitches excluded; horizontal RBSZ ±0.829 ft (17 in + ball radius 1.45 in); vertical from at-bat-specific sz_top/sz_bot.
- **Accuracy:** overall 85.5% (2008) → 89.3% (2016) → 91.9% (2023); true balls called correctly 92.9% → 96.8%; true strikes 73.1% → 90.1% (strike-side accuracy improved faster than ball-side, "to near parity"). "Vanishing lefty strike": correct ball calls off the outside edge to LHB 28% (2008) → 71% (2023). Count effects "persist: umpires still reluctant calling third strikes, but gap narrowed"; pitcher-handedness effects vanish after 2014.
- **Consistency:** convex-hull consistency (share of called balls outside the hull of called strikes) ≈0.950 → 0.985; α-convex-hull (α = 0.65 ft) ≈0.875 → 0.945; Mann–Kendall P < 0.00001.
- **Geometry (ATLAS 7-parameter asymmetric superellipse; boundary = NN P(strike) > 0.5):** half-width 1.106 ft (2008) → 0.907 (2023) vs RBSZ 0.829; half-height 0.860 → 1.001 vs 0.906; area 3.153 → 3.272 sq ft vs 3.004 (≈+9–10%); top rectangularity 0.976 → 1.448, bottom 1.466 → 1.985 (bottom "35–50% more rectangular" than top); centres x₀ −0.084 → 0.005 ft, y₀ 2.598 → 2.482 ft (RBSZ 2.508); shear ≈0. "By 2023 the CSZ's geometry aligned with that of the RBSZ in every respect except its size, which was still about 10% too large."
- **ABS discussion (quotable):** "an error rate still exceeding 8% after 15+ years… suggests need for automation"; at 8.2% misclassification and ~140 called pitches/game ⇒ "11.5 incorrect calls per game"; tennis line judges' ~8% error with 3 unsuccessful challenges per set is the analogy; TrackMan resolves in "<1 second"; a challenge system is "likely to be successful" and should be implemented "without delay"; if accuracy were much lower, "full automation preferable." Caveat they state: "Some of the perceived improvement may be due to improvements in the measurement system itself" (PITCHf/x→TrackMan vertical bounds), though geometry/consistency metrics also improved.
- **Relation:** the closest peer-reviewed antecedent to our accountability pillar (accuracy trend to 2023 = the pre-treatment baseline the AAA/spring/2026 shocks must be judged against) and to Pillar 6 (they *predict* challenge-system viability from an error rate; we measure it). Their "11.5 misses/game" is the population our tokens ration. Undone: no umpire IDs, no 2024–26, no Hawk-Eye truth, no player decisions.

### 2.8 Fesselmeyer, E. (2021). "The impact of temperature on labor quality: Umpire accuracy in Major League Baseball." *Southern Economic Journal* 88(2): 545–567, doi:10.1002/soej.12524 (online July 7, 2021; Monmouth Univ.; SSRN 3421241 dated 2019 lists Singapore Management Univ.; JEL Q51, J24, J81). [ABS+ — verbatim abstract via Crossref; Wiley/SSRN full text blocked]
- **Abstract (verbatim):** "Using data from Major League Baseball, I compute an objective measure of the home plate umpire's work quality—the accuracy of his ball and strike calls during a game—and measure how it varies with temperature. I find that an increase in game-time temperature from between 70 and 80°F to above 95°F decreases an umpire's accuracy by a little less than a percentage point, which is a 5.5% increase in the pitch-calling error rate when evaluated at the mean error rate of 13.3%. - **Relation:** an exogenous shifter of the umpire miss rate p — game-time temperature is in every Statcast/StatsAPI game feed — usable both as heterogeneity in the DP's p(state) and as an instrument-like source of variation in how many bad calls are available to challenge (does challenge *volume* rise on hot days while success stays flat?). ### 2.9 Shinya, M., & Tomomura, M. (2026). "Bayesian sensory integration explains ball-count bias in Major League Baseball umpires." Research Square preprint (Nature Portfolio, under review), posted June 10, 2026, doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-9598750/v1. CC BY 4.0. Hiroshima University. [FULL — PDF]
- **Data:** 450,460 called pitches, 2015–2024 regular + postseason (Baseball Savant), four-seam fastballs RHP→RHB only, complete location; umpire identity from Umpire Scorecards; 88 umpires in individual analyses. Horizontal RBSZ ±0.829 ft; vertical normalised with sz_top/sz_bot to 1.5–3.5 ft.
- **Method:** probit psychometric functions per boundary (outer/inner/high/low ROIs) × 12 counts; PSE = location with P(strike) = 0.5; σ = distance from PSE to P = 0.841; 1,000 bootstraps. Bayesian observer: x̂ ~ N(x, σₑ²), count-specific prior = two-component GMM of *called-pitch* locations (all-pitch prior rejected because it predicts same-direction shifts at inner and outer edges), posterior = precision-weighted mean, single threshold τ and σₑ held constant across counts, least-squares to observed PSEs.
- **Results:** ball-ahead counts → PSEs move toward centre (bigger zone), strike-ahead → outward; aggregate PSE sits outside the rulebook edge; model R²: high 0.940, outer 0.690, inner 0.515, low 0.241 (ground as visual anchor speculated); across umpires, higher σ ↔ larger flat-count PSE displacement (r = 0.235, p = 0.029) and larger count bias (r = 0.220, p = 0.041). Call-aversion rejected as sole account (shifts appear at 1-0/0-1); gambler's-fallacy sequential effects "not mutually exclusive."
- **Quotes:** "reframing count-dependent bias not as umpire error but as a computationally rational response to perceptual uncertainty" (Abstract). "From a strategic standpoint, defensive teams can expect higher challenge success rates in strike-ahead counts, while batters stand to gain more from challenging in ball-ahead counts" (Discussion). They propose "comparing pre- and post-2026 data" against hierarchical-Bayes predictions as "a powerful test."
- **Relation:** (i) supplies our count-conditional miss map q(count, edge) with a mechanism, and predicts *which side* finds overturnable calls in which counts — a testable implication for role × count success in 2026; (ii) their per-umpire σ (perceptual precision) is the umpire analogue of the σ_role we estimate for challengers; (iii) rival explanation for our accountability story: if count bias is rational integration, ABS feedback should shrink it only insofar as it changes umpires' priors or precision — state this. Undone: no framing/catcher, no 2026 data, one pitch type/handedness, low edge unexplained.

## 4. Claims we must NOT overclaim / traps

- **"Song et al. show umpires favoured stars pitch by pitch."** They show *performance* DiD on 148/112 player-seasons; status definition and pitch-level mechanism are not in the abstract. Say "consistent with removal of a status premium in called pitches."
- **"KBO 2024 = ABS effect."** 2024 also brought a shift ban (Cho & Lee's title) and pitch-clock trials; BB%/K% outcomes are more plausibly ABS-driven than OBP/BABIP. Any KBO comparison in Pillar 6 must name the bundle.
- **"Batters out-perceive pitchers (Chen et al.)."** Batters ≠ pitchers statistically (57 vs 55%; d′ 0.82 vs 0.63, ns); both > naive controls. Ecological validity is low (video occlusion, one pitcher, 120–125 km/h, umpire-view camera). Use as an ordering prior, never as σ magnitudes; our field estimates supersede.
- **"Chen et al. is fMRI evidence about challenges."** It is action *anticipation* before the plate, not judgment of a mid-plate plane after the pitch.
- **"Umpire accuracy is 92%" or "13.3% errors" or "8%."** Post et al. (91.9%, ±0.829 ft, sz_top/bot, 2023), Fesselmeyer (86.7%, PITCHf/x era, no buffer), and MLB's buffered grades differ by definition and era; always state zone, buffer, plane, years (see notes 22 trap 7).
- **"Count bias = umpire error the challenge system fixes."** Shinya & Tomomura (and Green & Daniels' own "Bayesian Instinct") make count dependence rational integration; describe as state-dependence and test whether ABS feedback moves priors/precision.
- **"The zone-outline literature says the called zone shrank in 2026 because of challenges."** Post et al. document a *rising* zone size to 2014 then plateau; 2026 changes must be differenced from the 2025 grading-buffer shock (notes 22 trap 4).
- **"Umpires' confidence tracks accuracy."** Chen & Huang: it does not.
- **"Post et al. proved the challenge system works."** They *argue* viability from error rates and a tennis analogy before 2026 data existed; we provide the test.
- Kamino/Wang trio: qualitative; do not lift their preference figures (54%/8%) without the primary survey.
- Cho & Lee and Song et al. full texts were not read here; do not quote beyond title/abstract.

## 5. Open questions this batch lets us answer

1. Does a *challenge* system remove star/status favouritism (Song et al.'s finding for full ABS), or only on challenged pitches? Test: star-batter called-strike differential in AAA full-ABS vs challenge days (2023–24), spring-2025 parks, and MLB 2025 vs 2026.
2. Are the count × side predictions of Shinya & Tomomura borne out in 2026 challenge success (defense wins more in strike-ahead counts; batters in ball-ahead)? And does the count-dependent PSE shift shrink under ABS accountability (prior update) or the σ shrink (precision), or neither?
3. Is the umpire-level σ from Shinya & Tomomura's psychometric fits predictive of how often an umpire gets challenged and overturned in 2026 (their 88-umpire σ is public via Umpire Scorecards IDs)?
4. Does temperature (Fesselmeyer) raise the supply of overturnable calls and hence challenge volume/timing without changing success — an exogenous check that players respond to the miss rate rather than to fixed habits?
5. Do the geometric residuals Post et al. leave in 2023 (zone ~10% too large; bottom edge more rectangular than top; lefty-strike remnant) map onto *where* successful 2026 challenges cluster (i.e., do challenges "prune" exactly the oversized margin)?
6. Role precision: with Hawk-Eye truth on every taken pitch, estimate σ_B, σ_C, σ_P and criterion c_role, and compare the ordering to Chen et al.'s lab ordering (B ≥ P) and to the umpire σ distribution.
7. Endogenous adaptation: replicate Song et al.'s player-status DiD on MLB 2025→2026 (challenge system) as a placebo/contrast to KBO's full ABS.
